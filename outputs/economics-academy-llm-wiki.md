Tier 1 'Draw the Map' (ages 10-12, sold as Pre-IGCSE): concepts discovered through games, simulations, stories — no exam content, no heavy calculation. 3. **Tiers are entry points, not a sequence.** A 13-15 year-old IB-bound beginner enters directly at Tier 2; Tier 1 is not a prerequisite. Never imply Pre-IGCSE must precede Pre-IB.

## §1 Purpose, Commitments & Architecture

**Purpose.** A premium foundation course bridging beginners into IGCSE and IB Economics thinking and real-world application. Assumes no prior economics. Built as one conceptual spine delivered through two thinking tiers — Tier 1 / Pre-IGCSE (ages 10-12) discovers concepts through stories, games and simulations; Tier 2 / Pre-IB (ages 13-15) promotes the same topics into definitions, diagrams, calculations and evaluative writing — with a swappable exam adapter fitted on top (see the 3-layer architecture below).

**Design commitment 1.** Intuition before theory. Each concept starts from a story, game or real situation, then becomes a definition and a diagram. Across the tiers this is the promotion mechanic: the same topic, only the verbs upgraded — Tier 1 feels it, Tier 2 judges it.

**Design commitment 2.** The exam adapter is drip-fed, never bolted on. Exam technique lives only in each lesson's Exam Adapter Slot (Tier 2), loaded lesson-by-lesson per the Exam Adapter Drip Map sheet: shared skills (definitions, diagrams, calculations) through M1, board-specific elements diverging from M2 L4, fully assembled by M3 L6. Sections 1-8 of every lesson stay exam-free.

**Design commitment 3.** The nine IB key concepts as a spine: scarcity, choice, efficiency, equity, economic well-being, sustainability, change, interdependence, intervention. [Verified against the current IB DP Economics guide, first assessment 2022]

**Design commitment 4.** Dual alignment with honest flags. Every lesson is anchored subtopic-by-subtopic to Cambridge IGCSE 0455 (2027-2029) and the IB DP Economics Guide (2022) — see the two audit sheets. Core content is bulleted in priority order and boundary-marked: [ENRICH] = beyond one or both syllabuses, [IB SL CORE] = core for IB-bound only, [LOW] = minor, keep light, (!) = scope note. Nothing outside the current syllabuses is ever taught as core.

**Design commitment 5.** One spine, single-point updates. Because both tiers hang off the same conceptual spine, a changing world requires exactly one edit: update the spine (a case, a news hook, a game) and it flows to both tiers automatically. This is what keeps the curriculum fluid in a fast-moving era without doubling maintenance.

**3-layer architecture (design principle).** This course does not split its tracks by exam board. It stacks a single conceptual spine, then two thinking tiers upon it — because the two boards share roughly 80% of their conceptual DNA, and a student at the point of entry does not yet know which board is their destination. Tier 1 'Draw the Map' (ages 10-12, the current Pre-IGCSE track): concepts discovered through games, simulations and stories, felt in the body before they are named. Tier 2 'Read the Map' (ages 13-15, the current Pre-IB track): the same topics, now carrying definitions, diagrams, calculations and evaluative writing. Layer 3, the exam adapter (not a separate module; fitted 1-2 years before the exam): the only layer where the boards genuinely diverge — an IGCSE adapter (3 AOs, no market-failure diagrams required) or an IB adapter (4 AOs, command terms, real-world-example essays) swaps on top of Tier 2. Each lesson's Exam-skill slot is the seed of this adapter layer. The public labels Pre-IGCSE / Pre-IB are retained, but internally these are thinking-stage tiers, not board tracks.

**Two tracks (delivery).** Tier 1 / Pre-IGCSE (ages 10-12): the same 18 topics, concrete and experiential — games, simulations, stories; simple 'trade-off curves' before formal PPCs; no heavy calculation. Tier 2 / Pre-IB (ages 13-15): full definitions, diagram discipline, AO-tagged objectives, calculations, command-term writing.

**Modules.** 1 - Microeconomics | 2 - Macroeconomics | 3 - Global Economics (6 lessons each). Independent, but recommended order is 1 -> 2 -> 3. v3 adds three lessons to close the audit gaps: M1 L5 Firms, Costs & Market Power; M2 L1 Money, Banking, Households & Workers; M3 L4 Inequality, Poverty & Population.

**Each module.** Lessons 1-5 teach; Lesson 6 = review + mock examination + exam-skills clinic (Tier 2 / Pre-IB) or capstone simulation + showcase quiz (Tier 1 / Pre-IGCSE). The three Lesson 6s are the exam adapter's assembly checkpoints.

**Framework basis.** IB side: current DP Economics guide (first assessment 2022; Tragakes 'Economics for the IB Diploma' structure): 4 syllabus units, 9 key concepts, 4 assessment objectives, Paper 1/2/3 formats. IGCSE side: Cambridge 0455 2027-2029 topics 1-6.

## §6 LESSON SPECS — the 18 lessons

### Module 1 — Microeconomics

#### M1L1 · The Economic Problem: Scarcity, Choice & Systems

**Core Concepts:**
- Scarcity
- Opportunity cost
- Factors of production
- Economic systems
- KC: Scarcity · Choice · Efficiency

**Essential Questions:**
1. Why can't anyone — even the richest country — have everything it wants?
2. What does every choice really cost?
3. Who should decide what gets produced: markets, governments, or both?

**Core content (priority-ordered):**
- Scarcity & the basic economic problem: finite resources vs infinite wants; needs vs wants; economic vs free goods
- Opportunity cost: definition; examples across consumers, workers, firms and governments; its role in every decision
- Factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise) and their rewards (rent, wages, interest, profit); causes of changes in their quantity & quality
- PPC: drawing & interpretation; points under/on/beyond the curve; movements along (= opportunity cost); shifts (= growth)
- The three resource-allocation questions: what / how / for whom
- Economic systems — market vs planned vs mixed: definitions and arguments for & against (IGCSE 2.8 / 2.10)

- [ENRICH · IB Unit 1] Economics as a social science; positive vs normative statements; methodology & pluralism
- [ENRICH · IB] Circular flow of income — not in the IGCSE syllabus, but essential scaffolding for macro in M2
- [LOW] Brief history of economic thought — one story, no depth

**Key diagrams:**
Production Possibilities Curve (PPC); circular flow model

**Exam-skill focus (Tier 2):**
AO1 command terms; how to define precisely; reading a PPC

**Tier 1 delivery — Draw the Map:**
Desert-island survival game: rank what to salvage, feel scarcity. 'Give up to get' trading round makes opportunity cost physical. Draw a simple 2-good 'trade-off curve' (pizza vs robots) instead of a formal PPC. Story of three villages (market / planned / mixed) — students vote on which they'd live in and defend it. 'Fact or opinion?' card sort seeds positive vs normative.

**Tier 2 delivery — Read the Map:**
Precise AO1 definitions; full PPC analysis (points on/inside/outside curve, shifts = growth); 2-sector circular flow; compare real economies (e.g. US / North Korea / Singapore) as systems; positive vs normative statements; first structured 'define + explain' written answers.

#### M1L2 · Demand, Supply & Market Equilibrium

**Core Concepts:**
- Demand & supply
- The price mechanism
- Equilibrium & disequilibrium
- KC: Efficiency · Interdependence · Change

**Essential Questions:**
1. How does a price 'know' how to change without anyone commanding it?
2. What is a price actually telling buyers and sellers?
3. What happens when a market is out of balance — and who fixes it?

**Core content (priority-ordered):**
- Law of demand & law of supply; individual vs market demand/supply; drawing & interpreting both diagrams
- Shifts OF a curve vs movements ALONG it — the single most common exam error; the full determinant taxonomy for each curve
- Market equilibrium & disequilibrium using BOTH schedules (tables) and curves; shortages & surpluses
- The price mechanism: how prices signal, ration and incentivise — answering what / how / for whom
- Causes & consequences of price changes; the effect on sales (feeds the revenue link in L3)

- [ENRICH · IB SL] Consumer & producer surplus — not required at IGCSE
- [ENRICH · IB HL 2.4] Behavioural-economics seed: biases, nudges, the critique of rational maximisation

**Key diagrams:**
D/S diagram; shifts vs movements along; equilibrium adjustment

**Exam-skill focus (Tier 2):**
Drawing & fully labelling D/S; distinguishing shift from movement

**Tier 1 delivery — Draw the Map:**
Live pit-market simulation: half the class buyers, half sellers with secret price cards — watch the price converge on its own. Sneaker-drop / concert-ticket stories for shortage. Plot the class's own trading data as the first 'demand curve'. No shifter taxonomy — just 'what would make you want more of this?' Bonus: a 'trick your brain' nudge experiment (behavioural econ seed).

**Tier 2 delivery — Read the Map:**
Full D/S diagram discipline (labels, arrows, equilibria e1->e2); complete determinant taxonomy; schedules AND curves; shift-vs-movement drills; consumer/producer surplus shading (IB enrichment); behavioural-econ enrichment discussion; write cause->effect chains using 'explain' and 'analyse'.

#### M1L3 · Elasticity

**Core Concepts:**
- Responsiveness (elasticity)
- PED & PES
- The revenue link
- KC: Change · Choice · Efficiency

**Essential Questions:**
1. Why do petrol prices swing wildly while haircut prices barely move?
2. When does raising your price actually lose you money?
3. How can a government use elasticity to change what we buy?

**Core content (priority-ordered):**
- PED: definition, formula, calculation with full working; the five-value vocabulary (perfectly inelastic -> inelastic -> unitary -> elastic -> perfectly elastic) with a diagram for each
- PED and revenue — the most-tested application: effect of price changes on consumer expenditure & firms' revenue, shown BOTH on a diagram and as a calculation
- Determinants of PED; significance for consumer, firm and government decisions
- PES: definition, formula, calculation, five-value interpretation, determinants

- [ENRICH · IB] YED: normal vs inferior goods; interpreting income elasticity
- [ENRICH · IB/HL] Tax-incidence intro: who really pays a tax depends on elasticity
- [LOW] XED — in NEITHER current syllabus (IB removed it in the 2022 guide; IGCSE never had it). Mention only if a student asks

**Key diagrams:**
Elastic vs inelastic curves; revenue effects

**Exam-skill focus (Tier 2):**
Calculations (show your working); interpreting values — maps to IGCSE AO2 / IB AO4

**Tier 1 delivery — Draw the Map:**
'Stretchy vs stiff' framing with a rubber band. Lemonade-stand pricing game: teams pick prices for water-in-a-desert vs one-of-many candy brands and discover revenue effects by playing. Sort real products onto an 'easy to give up <-> impossible to give up' line. Only PED intuition — no formulas.

**Tier 2 delivery — Read the Map:**
Full PED/PES/YED with calculations and sign interpretation; determinants; total-revenue rule proven numerically; tax-incidence intro (IB/HL enrichment); calculation technique — show working, interpret the value, state the implication for a firm or government.

#### M1L4 · Government Intervention & Market Failure

**Core Concepts:**
- Market failure
- Externalities & public goods
- Intervention & its limits
- KC: Intervention · Efficiency · Equity

**Essential Questions:**
1. When do markets fail — and how would we know?
2. Who pays for pollution if the polluter doesn't?
3. Does government intervention fix markets, or sometimes break them further?

**Core content (priority-ordered):**
- Market failure: definition + the full term set — public goods, merit & demerit goods, private / external / social costs & benefits, monopoly (newly listed as a market-failure term at IGCSE 2027-29)
- Causes & consequences of misallocation: over-consumption of demerit / external-cost goods; under-consumption of merit / external-benefit goods; non-provision of public goods; monopoly restricting supply and raising price
- Intervention on D/S diagrams (drawing REQUIRED at IGCSE): maximum & minimum prices, indirect taxes, subsidies — each with advantages & disadvantages
- The wider toolkit (definitions + pros/cons, no diagrams): regulation, privatisation & nationalisation, direct provision, quotas
- Market vs mixed economic system: arguments for and against each

- [ENRICH · IB] Externality & welfare-loss diagrams — NOT required at IGCSE 2027-29
- [ENRICH · IB] Common pool resources & the tragedy of the commons (the sustainability link)
- [LOW] Black-market consequences of price controls — one example, no depth

**Key diagrams:**
Price controls on D/S; tax & subsidy on D/S. Externality & welfare-loss diagrams = IB enrichment (NOT required for IGCSE 0455 2027-2029)

**Exam-skill focus (Tier 2):**
Intro to evaluation: discuss / evaluate; stakeholder analysis (IGCSE AO3 = evaluation, 10% weighting)

**Tier 1 delivery — Draw the Map:**
Factory-by-the-river role play: factory owner, fishermen, townspeople negotiate — externalities felt before named. Fishing-lake game: shared bowl of goldfish crackers depletes in rounds — tragedy of the commons discovered, not lectured. 'Free-rider' classroom experiment for public goods. Debate: 'Should candy be taxed like cigarettes?'

**Tier 2 delivery — Read the Map:**
Formal intervention toolkit on D/S diagrams (ceiling, floor, tax, subsidy) plus regulation/privatisation/nationalisation/direct provision/quotas with stakeholder tables (winners/losers); externality & CPR classification; merit/demerit/public goods and monopoly abuse with real policy cases; first full 'discuss' paragraph with a supported judgement.

#### M1L5 · Firms, Costs & Market Power  [NEW in v3]

**Core Concepts:**
- Costs, revenue & profit
- Economies of scale
- Competition vs monopoly
- KC: Efficiency · Choice · Interdependence

**Essential Questions:**
1. Why do some firms want to become huge — and when does big become a problem?
2. Where does profit actually come from?
3. Is one seller always worse for us than many?

**Core content (priority-ordered):**
- Costs of production: TC, ATC, FC, AFC, VC, AVC — definitions, calculations, and diagrams showing how costs change with output
- Revenue: TR & AR — definitions, calculations, and how sales volume drives revenue
- Economies & diseconomies of scale (internal AND external) with the ATC diagram — newly explicit at IGCSE 2027-29
- Objectives of firms: survival, social welfare, profit maximisation, growth
- Types of firms: sector (primary/secondary/tertiary), ownership (private/public), advantages & disadvantages of small vs large
- Mergers: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate — definitions, real examples, pros & cons
- Competitive vs monopoly markets: characteristics and effects on price, quality, choice, profit (NO diagrams or formal theory at IGCSE)
- Firms & production: demand for factors of production (product demand, factor prices, availability, productivity); labour- vs capital-intensive production; production vs productivity; effect of investment on productivity

- [ENRICH · IB HL 2.11] Market-structure spectrum preview (perfect competition -> monopoly)
- [ENRICH · IB HL 2.10] Asymmetric information: the 'lemons' problem

**Key diagrams:**
Average total cost (ATC) diagram for economies/diseconomies of scale (newly explicit in IGCSE 2027-29); cost-curve sketches. IB HL enrichment: market-structure spectrum

**Exam-skill focus (Tier 2):**
Cost & revenue calculations (show working); advantages/disadvantages tables; applying 'analyse' to a firm scenario

**Tier 1 delivery — Draw the Map:**
Cookie-factory simulation: teams 'produce' in rounds, hire more bakers and buy machines — feel average cost fall (economies of scale) then chaos as the factory floor jams (diseconomies). Merger negotiation round: two rival factories bargain — horizontal or vertical? 'Only lemonade stand in town' role play: the monopolist sets prices while the town reacts. Used-bikes 'lemons' mini-game as a teaser.

**Tier 2 delivery — Read the Map:**
Full cost/revenue vocabulary with calculations (TC/ATC/FC/AFC/VC/AVC, TR/AR); ATC diagram drawn to exam standard; merger types with real cases (e.g. Disney-Pixar vs a supermarket buying a farm); competitive-vs-monopoly comparison tables; structured 'analyse the effect on price, quality, choice and profit' answers; HL teaser on market structures.
