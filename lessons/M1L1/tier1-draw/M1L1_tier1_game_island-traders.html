🧠 For Tutors — Game Guide & Debrief
What this game teaches
Every item on the island has a hidden "survival value" the student can't see up front — just like real scarcity, they must decide under uncertainty with a raft that holds only 4 items out of 8. Round 2 turns "give up to get" into a felt mechanic: every accepted trade shows the student exactly what they gained and what they gave up, making opportunity cost visible the instant it happens.
Core mechanic
Sequential yes/no decisions under a hard capacity limit (raft = 4 slots). The student cannot "undo" a Grab or a Skip — mirroring the real economic problem that choices are rarely reversible.
Round structure
  • Round 1 — Salvage (8 items, 4 slots): items appear one at a time; Grab or Let it Sink. Ends when 4 are grabbed or all 8 are seen.
  • Round 2 — Trade (4 offers from Milo the Trader): accept or decline each swap. Accepting reveals the point gain/loss immediately.
  • Result: final survival score + a tier message (Master Survivor / Smart Trader / Room to Grow) tied to how well trade-offs were managed.
Pre-game brief (1 minute)
"You're shipwrecked. Your raft only holds 4 things — you'll see 8 float by, one at a time, and you can't go back for one you skipped. Later, a trader named Milo will offer you swaps. Every choice — grab, skip, trade, or decline — has a cost you can't take back. Watch for it."
Debrief questions
  • Which item did you skip that you now wish you'd grabbed — and why couldn't you take it too?
  • Pick one trade you accepted. What did you give up to get it — and was it worth it?
  • If your raft had held 6 slots instead of 4, would scarcity have disappeared, or just gotten smaller?
Common misconceptions & fix
"Opportunity cost is only about money." Point back to Round 1 — no money changed hands, yet every Grab still cost a raft slot. "More slots = no more scarcity." Ask what an 8-slot raft with 20 items floating by would feel like — scarcity returns at any size once wants exceed supply.
Variants
Simpler: raise the raft to 5 slots and read each item's value aloud before deciding. Harder: drop the raft to 3 slots and remove the value reveal on trades — students must estimate value from the item's description alone, closer to real market uncertainty.
