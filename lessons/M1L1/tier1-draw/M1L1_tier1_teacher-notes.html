Detailed Teaching Notes
Story → Discovery → Naming
  • Hook question: "Could even the richest country on Earth have everything it wants?" Let the class argue both sides for 2 minutes before revealing anything.
  • Desert island raft game: the physical constraint of only 3–4 items fitting does the teaching for you — let a student name the idea before you supply "finite resources vs. infinite wants."
  • "Give up to get" trading round: after every choice, ask "You got [X] — so what did you give up?"
  • Factors of production: use a school bake sale example — land = table space, labour = students baking, capital = the oven, enterprise = whoever had the idea and takes the risk.
  • Pizza-vs-robots trade-off curve: keep it a straight line, not a bowed PPC — that promotion happens at Tier 2.
  • Three villages vote: take a real show-of-hands before revealing reasons. No village is objectively "correct."

Trade-off curve: sketch a straight diagonal line, not a bowed curve — label only the two axis ends (e.g. "0 robots / 10 pizza" to "10 robots / 0 pizza").
Worked Example
Story: "Mia has $10 and wants both a $10 book and a $10 video game. She can only buy one. She buys the book. What's her opportunity cost?" Walk the class to the answer together: the opportunity cost is the video game — not the $10 (the money is just the tool used to make the choice; the opportunity cost is always the next-best alternative given up).
Common Misconceptions
"Scarcity just means poor." Fix: point out that even a billionaire has only 24 hours a day — time is scarce for everyone, rich or poor.
"Opportunity cost = the money spent." Fix: always ask "but what did you NOT get?" — the cost is the foregone alternative, not the price tag.
"More money = no more scarcity." Fix: use the "richest country" hook question again as a callback — wants grow as resources grow.

Child-Friendly Glossary
ScarcityNot having enough of something for everyone who wants it.
Opportunity costThe next-best thing you give up when you make a choice.
Factors of productionThe four ingredients needed to make anything: land, labour, capital, enterprise.
Economic systemThe way a group decides what gets made, how, and who gets it.
FactSomething that can be checked and proven true.
OpinionSomeone's feeling or judgement — it can't be proven right or wrong.
