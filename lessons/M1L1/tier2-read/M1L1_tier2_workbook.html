Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. Opportunity cost: the value of the next-best alternative foregone when a choice is made. [1 mark definition] + [2 marks for a correctly applied real example that clearly names both the choice made and the next-best alternative given up]
2. A bowed-outward (concave to origin) curve; A anywhere on the curve; B anywhere inside (underutilisation of resources); C anywhere outside (currently unattainable). [1 mark correct curve shape, 1 mark each for correctly placed A/B/C]
3. A→B: a movement along/inside the curve — represents underutilised resources or a choice to produce less of both goods than possible (not itself opportunity cost unless comparing two points ON the curve). An outward shift: economic growth — the economy's productive capacity has increased (e.g. more/better factors of production, new technology). [Accept: movement along the curve itself, e.g. A to another on-curve point, represents opportunity cost — award marks for correct reasoning even if framing differs slightly]
4. The opportunity cost is the hospital that was not built — specifically the healthcare capacity/services foregone. Most-affected stakeholder: accept any well-justified group, e.g. patients/lower-income citizens who rely on public healthcare (as opposed to commuters/businesses who benefit from the highway). [2 marks for correctly identifying the foregone alternative in specific terms, not just "the other option"; 2 marks for a stakeholder with justification]
5. Band 3 (5–6 marks) answers should: define scarcity briefly, then explain that a market system still leaves some wants unmet because resources go to those with purchasing power (not all wants); a planned system still leaves wants unmet because a central authority cannot perfectly know or satisfy every want, and is constrained by the same finite factors of production; a strong answer notes both systems face the SAME underlying constraint — finite factors of production versus infinite wants — they merely allocate the scarcity differently, they do not eliminate it. Band 2 (3–4): one system explained well, second underdeveloped. Band 1 (1–2): describes systems without linking back to scarcity as the persistent constraint.
