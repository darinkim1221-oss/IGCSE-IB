Warm-up
Show what you discovered today
1. In your own words, what does it mean when a factory's pollution is "a cost that spills onto other people"?
2. Fill in the blank: A good spillover that benefits people who never paid for it is called a ______________ spillover.
3. Why can a shared fishing lake "collapse" even though no single boat WANTS that to happen? Explain in 1–2 sentences.
4. In the Fishing Lake game, what happened when you fished at HIGH effort round after round? Why did it happen?
5. True or False: If you can enjoy something without paying for it, most people will still choose to pay for it anyway.
6. Match each example to POSITIVE or NEGATIVE spillover.
A beekeeper's bees pollinate the neighbor's crops for free____________
Factory smoke drifting over a nearby town____________
Getting vaccinated, which also protects others nearby____________
Litter left behind after a picnic in a shared park____________
7. Name TWO tools a government could use to fix a spillover problem or protect a shared resource.
8. A city builds a free public park. Explain why some people might choose not to help pay for its upkeep, using the word "free-ride" in your answer.
9. Should candy be taxed like cigarettes? Give ONE argument FOR and ONE argument AGAINST.
10. How could a government use taxes, bans, or rules to protect a shared resource like a fishing lake from collapsing?
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Can you think of a time something someone else did affected you for free — either a benefit or a cost — without you ever agreeing to it?" Write down their example and say whether it was a positive or negative spillover.
Answer Key
1. Accept any answer capturing: the factory's decision to pollute creates a real cost (dirty water, fewer fish, health problems) for people who never agreed to it and never got paid for it — the cost "spills" outside the factory's own books.
2. Positive. — A positive spillover (positive externality) benefits someone who didn't pay for it, like a beautiful garden the whole street enjoys.
3. Sample: "Nobody owns the fish until it's caught, so every boat has a reason to catch as many as it can right now — but if every boat thinks that way at the same time, the shared stock can be wiped out faster than it can regrow." Accept reasoning built around "nobody owns it, so nobody alone holds back."
4. Fishing HIGH every round catches more per round short-term but drains the shared stock fast — usually causing the lake to collapse within the first few rounds, ending catches for the rest of the season for everyone, including the player.
5. False. — If nobody can be stopped from enjoying something without paying (like a free public fireworks show), many people will choose to "free-ride" and let others pay instead.
6. Beekeeper's bees: Positive. Factory smoke: Negative. Vaccination: Positive. Litter: Negative.
7. Accept any two: a tax (make the bad thing more expensive), a subsidy (make the good thing cheaper), a ban or regulation (stop or limit it), direct provision (the government provides it itself), or a quota/rule limiting use of a shared resource.
8. Accept: because nobody can be stopped from using the park even if they didn't pay, some people "free-ride" — they enjoy the benefit while letting others cover the cost, which can mean the park doesn't get enough money for its upkeep.
9. Accept any reasonable pair, e.g. FOR: "it would raise money and might reduce how much sugary candy kids eat, protecting health." AGAINST: "candy isn't as dangerous as cigarettes, and a tax mostly hurts families with less money to spend."
10. Sample: "A government could set a fishing quota (a rule limiting how many fish each boat can catch), tax overfishing to make it more expensive, or ban fishing during certain seasons so the lake has time to regrow." Accept any answer connecting a government tool to protecting a shared, unowned resource from being overused.
