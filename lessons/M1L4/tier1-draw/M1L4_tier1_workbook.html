Warm-up
Show what you discovered today
1. What is a "spillover" (externality)? Circle the best answer.
  • A price that is too high.
  • A cost or benefit from a decision that lands on someone who wasn't part of making it.
  • Money a government collects from taxes.
2. Fill in the blank: A good spillover that benefits people who never paid for it is called a ______________ spillover.
3. Match each example to POSITIVE or NEGATIVE spillover.
A beekeeper's bees pollinate the neighbor's crops for free
Factory smoke drifting over a nearby town
Getting vaccinated, which also protects others nearby
Litter left behind after a picnic in a shared park
4. Why can a shared fishing lake "collapse" even though no single boat WANTS that to happen? Explain in 1–2 sentences.
5. Draw a bar graph. Draw one bar for how many fish were in the river BEFORE the factory started producing, and one bar for how many were left AFTER several rounds of "Produce More."

6. Which tool would you use to fix a factory's water pollution? Circle ONE, then give ONE reason.
  • Tax the factory for every unit it pollutes
  • Ban the pollution completely
7. A city builds a free public park. Explain why some people might choose not to help pay for its upkeep, using the word "free-ride" in your answer.
8. Should candy be taxed like cigarettes? Give ONE argument FOR and ONE argument AGAINST.
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Can you think of a time something someone else did affected you for free — either a benefit or a cost — without you ever agreeing to it?" Write down their example and say whether it was a positive or negative spillover.
Answer Key
1. "A cost or benefit from a decision that lands on someone who wasn't part of making it." — An externality spills outside the deal between buyer and seller.
2. Positive. — A positive spillover (positive externality) benefits someone who didn't pay for it, like a beautiful garden the whole street enjoys.
3. Beekeeper's bees: Positive. Factory smoke: Negative. Vaccination: Positive. Litter: Negative.
4. Sample: "Nobody owns the fish until it's caught, so every boat has a reason to catch as many as it can right now — but if every boat thinks that way at the same time, the shared stock can be wiped out faster than it can regrow." Accept reasoning built around "nobody owns it, so nobody alone holds back."
5. Accept any two bars showing far fewer fish after several rounds of "Produce More" — the key skill is drawing/reading a simple bar graph.
6. Accept any reasoned answer — check the reason actually matches the choice (e.g. "Tax, because the factory can keep operating but pays for the damage" or "Ban, because some pollution is too dangerous to allow at any price").
7. Accept: because nobody can be stopped from using the park even if they didn't pay, some people "free-ride" — they enjoy the benefit while letting others cover the cost, which can mean the park doesn't get enough money for its upkeep.
8. Accept any reasonable pair, e.g. FOR: "it would raise money and might reduce how much sugary candy kids eat, protecting health." AGAINST: "candy isn't as dangerous as cigarettes, and a tax mostly hurts families with less money to spend."
