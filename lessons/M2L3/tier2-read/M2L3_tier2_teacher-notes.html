Detailed Teaching Notes
Scaffolding for IB, not IGCSE examined content
  • Critical framing: state explicitly, up front, that the AD-AS model is IB content and does NOT appear anywhere in IGCSE 0455 (2027-29). For IGCSE-bound students this lesson is conceptual scaffolding for the verbal "total demand/total supply" reasoning IGCSE actually tests in M2L4/M2L5.
  • Hook: "every event in an economy moves one shared total" — frame the lesson as testing whether every shock can be drawn as a shift of AD or AS.
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Explain / Compare before the tabs appear — "compare" demands BOTH AS views, not just one.
  • Components of AD tabs: C is usually largest, I is most volatile — push students past just naming the four letters to explaining what shifts each one.
  • AS shapes tabs: the Keynesian/monetarist disagreement is not just academic — it changes the POLICY conclusion, which is exactly why this lesson bridges into M2L5's demand-side vs. supply-side debate.
  • AD-AS diagram & output gaps: drill the sequence every time — draw AD and AS, mark equilibrium, draw the vertical full-employment output line, then identify whether equilibrium sits left (negative gap) or right (positive gap) of it.
Visuals & Diagrams
📉
The AD-AS diagram: downward-sloping AD, upward-sloping (Keynesian-style, three-zone) AS, crossing at equilibrium, with a dashed vertical full-employment output line used to identify output gaps. This diagram is IB-level content — NOT required anywhere in IGCSE 0455.
Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Examine whether the Keynesian or monetarist/new-classical view of aggregate supply better explains what happens when a government increases spending during a recession." Model: CLAIM — the Keynesian view fits a deep recession with spare capacity better. EVIDENCE — with high unemployment, a flat Keynesian AS lets extra AD raise real output significantly with limited inflation; a vertical monetarist AS predicts mostly inflation regardless of spare capacity. LINK — conclude the Keynesian view is more convincing specifically in a recession, while the monetarist view may better describe an economy already near full employment. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q5.
Common Misconceptions
"This is IGCSE exam content." Fix: state explicitly and repeatedly that AD-AS is IB-only — IGCSE tests the underlying verbal reasoning chain, not this diagram.
"AD-AS diagrams use 'Price' and 'Quantity' like a normal market diagram." Fix: drill "Price Level" and "Real Output" every time — these describe the WHOLE economy, not one market, and examiners penalise P/Q labelling here.
"One economist is simply right and the other is wrong." Fix: use the "why it matters" tab — the Keynesian/monetarist disagreement changes the recommended POLICY, and both views remain influential in real economic debate.

Exam-Precise Glossary
Aggregate demand (AD)Total demand for all goods and services in an economy at a given price level: C + I + G + (X − M).
Aggregate supply (AS)The total quantity of goods and services producers are willing to supply at each price level.
Macroeconomic equilibriumThe price level and real output where AD and AS cross.
Output gapThe difference between actual real output and full-employment (potential) output — negative (below) or positive (above).
