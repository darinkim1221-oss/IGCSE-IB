Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. Aggregate demand is the total demand for all goods and services in an economy at a given price level. Components: Consumption (C), Investment (I), Government spending (G), Net exports (X − M). [1 mark definition, 2 marks for all four components correctly named]
2. Keynesians draw AS as flat at low output (spare capacity means output can rise with little price rise), then vertical at full employment. Monetarists/new-classical economists draw AS as vertical (or near-vertical) even in the relevant time frame, since output is determined by real resources, not the price level — so a rise in AD is mostly inflationary. [2 marks per correctly explained view]
3. AD and AS crossing at an equilibrium output level BELOW a dashed vertical full-employment output line, with the negative gap (the horizontal distance between equilibrium output and full-employment output) clearly labelled. [1 mark correct AD/AS shapes, 1 mark full-employment output line labelled, 2 marks negative gap correctly identified and labelled]
4. Higher government spending is a direct rise in the G component of AD, so the AD curve shifts right. Depending on where the economy sits on the AS curve: if there is spare capacity (Keynesian flat zone), real output rises with little price rise; if the economy is near or at full employment (vertical AS), the same AD rise causes mostly a rise in the price level with little extra real output. [2 marks correct AD shift explained, 2 marks correct conditional reasoning about the AS zone]
5. Band 3 (6–8 marks): a clear CLAIM (e.g. "the Keynesian view better explains a DEEP recession with spare capacity"), EVIDENCE (in a recession with high unemployment, spare capacity means a Keynesian flat AS allows government spending to raise real output significantly with limited inflation; a monetarist vertical AS would predict mostly inflation regardless of spare capacity) and a LINK back to the question (a supported judgement that the Keynesian view fits a recession scenario better, while the monetarist view may fit an economy already near full employment). Band 2 (3–5): correct concept named but the claim-evidence-link chain is incomplete. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion with no named diagram concept.
