Warm-up
Show what you discovered this module
1. In one or two sentences, tell the "whole story" of this module — from scarcity all the way to firms — in your own words.
2. Fill in the blank: what you give up when you make a choice is called the ______________ ______________.
3. True or False: when the PRICE of a good changes, the whole demand curve shifts to a new position. Explain your answer.
4. A hot day causes an ice-cream stand to raise its price, but people still buy nearly the same amount of ice cream. Is this demand ELASTIC or INELASTIC? Explain in one sentence.
5. In the Market Day game, what happened to your stall's cost per cup on the Sales Tax day? What happened on the Subsidy day?
6. What is the difference between a price CEILING and a price FLOOR? Give one real-world example of each.
7. On the Monopoly Licence day of the game, what changed for your stall if you WON the auction? What changed if you LOST it?
8. Match each idea to the KEY CONCEPT it connects to most.
Opportunity cost____________

Economies of scale____________
Choices: Efficiency · Choice · Interdependence
9. True or False: "cost" and "price" mean the same thing. Explain your answer.
10. Which of the six market days in the Market Day game did you find most surprising, and why? Connect your answer to a concept from this module.
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Has a government rule — a tax, a subsidy, or a price control — ever affected something you bought or sold?" Write down what happened, and name which type of rule it was.
Answer Key
1. Accept any reasonable summary that connects the ideas in order, e.g. "Because resources are scarce, people and businesses must choose; those choices meet in markets where prices balance demand and supply; how much quantity changes when price changes is elasticity; sometimes the government steps in with taxes, subsidies, or price rules; and firms decide how big to grow, whether to merge, and how much power they have over price." Full marks for any version that touches scarcity/choice, prices, elasticity, government, and firms in a connected way.
2. Opportunity cost. — The next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
3. False. — A price change causes a MOVEMENT ALONG the same demand curve, not a shift. The curve itself only shifts when something else changes, like income or tastes.
4. Inelastic. — Buyers keep buying almost the same amount even though the price rose, which means quantity demanded barely responds to price — the definition of inelastic demand.
5. On the Sales Tax day, cost per cup went UP (the tax adds to the stall's cost). On the Subsidy day, cost per cup went DOWN (the government payment reduces the stall's cost). A tax and a subsidy move a firm's cost in opposite directions.
6. A price CEILING is a legal MAXIMUM price (e.g. rent control, so landlords can't charge more than a set amount). A price FLOOR is a legal MINIMUM price (e.g. a minimum wage, so employers can't pay less than a set amount). Ceilings protect buyers; floors protect sellers.
7. Winning made you the ONLY legal seller that day — you could usually charge more, since customers who still wanted a cup had nowhere else to go. Losing meant a rival stall held exclusive rights, so most customers went there instead and your stall had a much quieter day. This is the felt experience of being on either side of a monopoly.
8. Opportunity cost: Choice. Externality: Interdependence. Economies of scale: Efficiency.
9. False. — COST is what it takes a firm to make something (materials, wages, rent). PRICE is what a buyer pays for it. A firm sets its price based on cost plus the profit it wants to make.
10. Accept any reasonable answer that names one of the six days (Free Market, Sales Tax, Subsidy, Price Ceiling, Price Floor, or Monopoly Licence Auction) and correctly connects it to its concept (see the game's Concept Recap for the exact wording).
