🧠 For Tutors — Game Guide & Debrief
What this game teaches
This is the Module 1 capstone — every mechanic maps directly to a concept from Lessons 1–5. Students run a snack stall across 6 "market days," each with a different rule imposed by "the government": a free market baseline, a sales tax, a subsidy, a price ceiling, a price floor, and a Monopoly Licence auction. By choosing a price each day and watching how customers, costs, and profit respond, students feel — rather than just recall — how each type of intervention changes a market, and what it means to be the only seller in town.
Core mechanic
Each round shows an event banner (the day's rule) and up to three price buttons — Low $0.80, Fair $1.20, High $1.80. The game computes how many cups sell (fewer at high prices, more at low prices — elasticity in action) and the profit per cup after that day's cost or price rule. A price ceiling round removes the High price option (illegal to charge that much); a price floor round removes the Low price option (illegal to charge that little); a tax round raises cost; a subsidy round lowers cost. On the Monopoly Licence day, students first bid (Low/Med/High) for the exclusive right to be the only seller — a bigger bid costs more up front but wins the licence more often, and winning dramatically changes how many customers show up.
Round structure
  • Day 1 — Free Market: baseline, no rules, pure supply and demand.
  • Day 2 — Sales Tax: a $0.30 per-cup tax raises the stall's cost.
  • Day 3 — Subsidy: a $0.20 per-cup government payment lowers the stall's cost.
  • Day 4 — Price Ceiling: a legal maximum price of $1.20 removes the High option.
  • Day 5 — Price Floor: a legal minimum price of $1.20 removes the Low option.
  • Day 6 — Monopoly Licence Auction: bid for exclusive selling rights, then price knowing whether you won or lost the licence.
  • Result: total profit across all 6 days, plus a Concept Recap mapping every day's event to the economic idea it demonstrated.
Pre-game brief (1 minute)
"You run a snack stall. Most days, you just pick a price and see who buys. But this is a review week, so the town government keeps changing the rules — taxes, subsidies, price limits — and on the last day, they're even auctioning off the right to be the ONLY seller in town. Watch what each rule does to your customers and your profit, and be ready to explain why."
Debrief questions
  • On the tax day versus the subsidy day, how did your best price choice change? Why?
  • On the price ceiling day, what option disappeared — and who does a price ceiling usually protect?
  • If you won the Monopoly Licence, what changed about how many customers you had and what price you could charge? If you lost, what did that feel like?
  • Which single day taught you the most? Map it back to the lesson (L1–L5) it came from.
Common misconceptions & fix
"A tax and a price ceiling are the same kind of rule." A tax changes the stall's COST (economists model this as a leftward shift in supply); a price ceiling directly limits what price is legally allowed — have students name the difference out loud before moving on. "Winning a monopoly is only good news." Ask a student who won the licence what happened to buyers' choices that day — point out that while the seller gains power, buyers usually lose out on price and choice, which is exactly what Lesson 5's lemonade-stand role play showed.
Variants
Simpler: play only Days 1, 2, and 4 (free market, tax, ceiling) for a shorter 3-round version focused on government intervention alone. Harder: after finishing, have students redesign one day's rule (e.g. a bigger tax, a stricter ceiling) and predict — before replaying — how their best price choice would change.
