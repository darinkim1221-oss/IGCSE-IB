Detailed Teaching Notes
From recall to judgement
  • Hook: two students who know exactly the same facts can still earn very different marks — the gap is HOW they write, not WHAT they know. State this explicitly: no new economics today.
  • Command-term drill: separates discuss (present more than one side), evaluate (present more than one side AND weigh them to reach a judgement), and to what extent (state a degree/condition, not a flat yes/no) — the three most conflated command terms across both boards.
  • MCQ warm-up: deliberately drawn from across the whole module — treat wrong answers as a diagnostic for which earlier lesson needs a 2-minute verbal recap.
  • Short-answer mark-scheme literacy: uses an ATC calculation to show marks are awarded for the FORMULA, the SUBSTITUTION, and the labelled FINAL ANSWER as three separate credit-bearing steps.
  • Data-response practice: rehearses the (a)/(b)/(c) escalating-depth structure common to both boards — model reading the WHOLE question stem before writing any answer.
  • Levels of response: put a genuine Band 1 and Band 3 answer to the SAME 8-mark question side by side and have students name the differences themselves before revealing the mark-scheme language (structure / content / judgement).
  • Evaluation paragraph: walks CLAIM → ANALYSIS → COUNTER → JUDGEMENT using a bakery-merger example from M1L5. The COUNTER is the step students skip most under time pressure — without it, the JUDGEMENT collapses into a restated claim.
  • IB "discuss" structure: a first rehearsal of the mini Part (b) format — two genuinely distinct sides, then a supported judgement in one or two sentences, NOT a full essay.
  • Misconception clinic: re-framed around "why this specific wording costs marks" — e.g. "demand falls" instead of "quantity demanded falls" may be read as a shift/movement confusion and lose the mark.
  • Timing & strategy: roughly one minute per mark as a pacing check; a 30–60 second plan before any extended response; let the command word set the depth.
  • Concept connections recap: re-walks L1–L2, L3–L4, and L5 as three connected movements in one story.
  • Positive/normative game: a module-wide review of the positive/normative distinction from L2 and L4, applied to statements spanning the whole module.

Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Evaluate whether a monopoly is always harmful to consumers." Model: CLAIM — a monopoly is not automatically harmful; its effect depends on how it uses its market power. ANALYSIS — a monopoly can charge a higher price and offer less choice than a competitive market (link to price/choice, M1L5). COUNTER — a monopoly can also achieve economies of scale a fragmented market cannot, and if regulated (a price ceiling, M1L4) some of that cost saving can reach consumers; guaranteed long-run profit can also fund innovation. JUDGEMENT — harmful when barriers to entry are high and unregulated; less harmful, potentially beneficial, when scale economies are passed on or intervention constrains pricing power. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q10.
Common Misconceptions
"Discuss and evaluate mean the same thing — just write everything I know." Fix: discuss only requires presenting more than one side; evaluate additionally requires weighing those sides to reach a supported judgement.
"Price rises so demand falls" (written in an exam answer). Fix: this describes a MOVEMENT along the demand curve — the correct phrase is "quantity demanded falls."
"Cost and price are the same word for the same thing." Fix: cost is what it takes a FIRM to produce something; price is what a BUYER pays.

Exam-Precise Glossary
Command termThe instruction word in a question (e.g. define, explain, discuss, evaluate) that fixes exactly what kind of response is required and how much depth earns credit.
Levels of responseAn IGCSE mark-scheme format that grades an extended answer holistically into bands (e.g. Band 1–3) based on structure, content, and judgement, rather than awarding isolated single marks.
Claim → Analysis → Counter → JudgementThe four-part scaffold for a full evaluation paragraph: a clear position, the reasoning/mechanism behind it, a genuine opposing consideration, and a final supported (usually conditional) conclusion.
"Discuss" (IB)Requires presenting more than one side of an issue; does not by itself require a final judgement (that is "evaluate" or "to what extent").
"To what extent" (IB)Requires a judgement stated as a DEGREE or CONDITION, not a flat yes/no — e.g. "largely… but only when…".
Movement vs. shiftA price change causes a movement ALONG a demand or supply curve; a change in any other factor (income, tastes, related-good prices) causes the whole curve to SHIFT.
