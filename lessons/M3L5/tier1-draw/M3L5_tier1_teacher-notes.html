Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel the difference between two lives before defining development
  • Hook: "two babies born on the same day" — let students predict what might differ between their lives before revealing the contrast in access to schools, hospitals, water, and electricity.
  • Why are some countries richer? tabs: keep the causes concrete (jobs/industries, education, healthcare, natural resources) — this deliberately echoes the "international differences" causes already introduced in M3L4, now applied specifically to development.
  • What is globalisation? tabs: use the phone/clothes example (parts from many countries) to make the definition concrete before naming any effects.
  • Town Hall role play: this is a DISCOVERY activity — let students hear all four voices (worker, shop owner, government, environment) before asking whether the company should be allowed in. There is no single "correct" answer; the goal is recognising that MNCs bring BOTH benefits and costs.
  • Country Detective: keep the three clues (GDP per person, years of school, life expectancy) together every round — the point is that development is judged using MULTIPLE indicators, not income alone.
  • Sustainability tabs: keep this light and forward-looking — the goal is planting the idea, not delivering a full climate-economics lesson.

Worked Example
Story: "Two babies are born on the same day, in two different countries. Baby A grows up near good schools and hospitals, with electricity and clean water every day. Baby B grows up walking far to fetch water, and their school often runs short on teachers and books." Ask: what could explain a difference like this? Guide students toward jobs/industries, education, healthcare, and natural resources as reasonable, non-judgemental explanations — never framed as any one country or people being "better."
Common Misconceptions
"A country is developed just because it's rich." Fix: use Country Detective — a country can have a decent income but still lag in schooling or healthcare, and development looks at ALL of these together.
"Multinational companies are always good — or always bad — for a country." Fix: use the Town Hall role play — every voice has a reasonable point; MNCs bring real benefits (jobs, tax revenue) AND real costs (competition for local business, environmental pressure).
"Globalisation is a brand-new thing." Fix: while the pace has sped up hugely with modern technology, countries trading and connecting with each other is not new — what's new is how fast and how much is now connected.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
Economic growthAn increase in a country's total output over time.
Economic developmentAn overall improvement in living standards — health, education, and income together.
GDP per personA country's total income divided by its population.
GlobalisationCountries, businesses, and cultures becoming more connected worldwide.
SustainabilityMeeting today's needs without making it harder for future generations to meet theirs.
