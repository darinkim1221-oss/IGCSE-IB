Detailed Teaching Notes
Full syllabus rigor — the widest-ranging lesson in Module 3
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Explain / Justify first — "justify" previews the strategy-recommendation reasoning needed at the end of the module.
  • Growth vs Development & HDI tabs: stress that growth is necessary but NOT sufficient for development — this single sentence pre-empts a very common exam error.
  • Development Indicators diagram: the KEY visual for this lesson — drill the point that GDP per person, HDI, life expectancy and years of schooling usually move together, but NOT identically, which is exactly why multiple indicators matter.
  • Causes of international development differences tabs: eight tabs combining ALL of the syllabus's named causes — treat this as the most exam-relevant content in the lesson for AO1 recall questions.
  • Globalisation tabs: definition first, then the four named drivers (falling trade restrictions, transport costs, communication costs, MNC movement) — students often conflate "globalisation" with just "free trade," so keep the definition broad.
  • Effects of globalisation tabs: six effects, deliberately two-sided within each tab where possible (e.g. competition drives innovation but also closes local firms) — this is the AO2 backbone of the lesson.
  • MNC host/home tabs: the four-way host/home × advantage/disadvantage grid is the single most exam-testable structure in this lesson — drill it as a 2×2, not four separate facts.
  • Poverty cycle diagram: drill the FULL sequence every time — low income → low savings/investment → low productivity → low output → (loops back). Debt and weak institutions are framed as things that can trap a country IN this cycle.
  • Sustainability tabs: reflect the 2027–29 syllabus's integration of sustainability as a cross-cutting theme, not an isolated topic — link back to earlier lessons where relevant (e.g. externalities, if already covered).
  • IB HL enrichment: flag clearly that the integration ladder, WTO/IMF/World Bank, and named development strategies are NOT required at IGCSE 0455.

Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Evaluate whether free trade promotes economic development." Model: CLAIM — free trade can promote development but is not sufficient on its own. EVIDENCE — free trade gives countries access to larger export markets and can drive specialisation gains, potentially raising incomes and funding further development (as in export-led growth strategies); however, countries with weak infrastructure or institutions may struggle to compete even with open trade access, and some domestic industries can be harmed by import competition before new export industries develop. LINK — free trade is likely to help development most when paired with investment in infrastructure, education and institutions; trade openness alone does not guarantee development. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q6.
Common Misconceptions
"Growth and development are the same thing." Fix: use the Development Indicators diagram — a country can grow (rising GDP) without its HDI rising much, if the gains aren't invested in health and education.
"Multinational companies are simply good, or simply bad, for a country." Fix: use the MNC host/home 2×2 grid — every combination has a real, explainable trade-off; there is no single correct verdict independent of context.
"A country in the poverty cycle just needs to try harder to save more." Fix: the poverty cycle is a STRUCTURAL trap — very low incomes leave little room to save even with effort, which is precisely why outside investment, aid, or a policy shock is often needed to break it.

Exam-Precise Glossary
Human Development Index (HDI)A composite index (0 to 1) combining life expectancy, years of schooling, and GNI per capita.
GlobalisationThe growing economic, social and cultural interconnection of countries worldwide.
Multinational company (MNC)A company that owns or controls production in more than one country.
Poverty cycleA self-reinforcing loop of low income, low savings/investment, low productivity, and low output.
SustainabilityMeeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own.
Integration ladder (IB)The stages of trade-bloc depth: preferential agreement → free trade area → customs union → common market → monetary union.
