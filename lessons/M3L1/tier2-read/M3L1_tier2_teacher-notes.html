Detailed Teaching Notes
From intuition to numerical proof
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Explain / Comment before the tabs — "comment" previews the balanced reasoning needed for the extended response.
  • Specialisation tabs: stress that the "basis" (resource allocation / low-cost production) is WHY a country specialises, distinct from the advantages/disadvantages of doing so.
  • Free trade tabs: keep this genuinely balanced — every "advantage" should have a matching "disadvantage" a student could cite in an 8-mark discuss question.
  • Gains from trade calculator: drill the sequence every time — BEFORE (split production) totals vs. AFTER (full specialisation) totals, using the SAME total labour hours. The "world total rises" result is the entire payoff of this lesson — make sure every student can restate it in their own words.
  • IB HL enrichment: flag clearly that absolute vs. comparative advantage and opportunity-cost tables are NOT required at IGCSE 0455. The key insight for IB-track students: gains from trade depend on COMPARATIVE (relative) advantage, not absolute advantage — this is often the single most confused concept in this topic.

Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Discuss whether free trade always benefits every country that takes part." Model: CLAIM — free trade does not automatically benefit every country equally. EVIDENCE — consumers and efficient exporters gain from lower prices and larger markets, but domestic industries unable to compete with cheaper imports can decline, causing structural unemployment in those specific sectors. LINK — conclude that the country as a whole may gain, but the gains and losses are not evenly spread across every group within it. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q6.
Common Misconceptions
"A country with an absolute advantage in everything has nothing to gain from trade." Fix: use the IB HL opportunity-cost table — even a country that is absolutely better at everything still has a LOWER opportunity cost in one good than the other, giving it comparative advantage there.
"Specialisation and free trade are the same thing." Fix: specialisation is a PRODUCTION decision (what a country chooses to make); free trade is a POLICY decision (whether goods can cross borders without taxes or limits).
"Gains from trade mean nobody loses." Fix: the WORLD total rises, but within each country there can be winners (consumers, competitive exporters) and losers (uncompetitive domestic producers and their workers).

Exam-Precise Glossary
SpecialisationA country focusing on producing what it makes best or cheapest, based on best resource allocation and/or low-cost production.
Free tradeTrade between countries with no taxes or limits placed on what crosses the border.
Gains from tradeThe increase in total world output that results when countries specialise according to their strengths and trade, compared to each trying to produce everything alone.
Absolute advantageProducing MORE of a good than another country using the same resources.
Comparative advantageHaving a LOWER opportunity cost of producing a good than another country.
Terms of tradeHow much of one good exchanges for another between trading countries — determines how the gains from trade are shared.
