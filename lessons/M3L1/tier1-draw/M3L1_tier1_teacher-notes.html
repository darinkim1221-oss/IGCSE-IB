Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel the gains from trade before naming them
  • Hook: "could your country make everything itself?" — let both sides argue before revealing the trading game.
  • Trading Card Economy: Team Blue is cheap at Fruit + Fabric; Team Gold is cheap at Tech + Toys. Round 1 (No Trade) caps both teams at 2 kinds of goods; Round 2 (Free Trade) lets both reach all 4. The score jump IS the lesson — no team had to learn a new skill to get richer.
  • Messi puzzle: the key insight is OPPORTUNITY COST, not ability — Messi could out-mow most gardeners, but his time is worth far more elsewhere. This is the plain-English seed of comparative advantage, without naming the formal theory or opportunity-cost tables (that's Tier 2).
  • Backpack Map: ground the abstract idea in items students are physically carrying — this is specialisation happening in their own lives, right now.
  • Advantages/disadvantages tabs: keep this balanced and concrete — every "upside" tab should have a genuine "downside" tab a student could reasonably raise in a discussion later.

Worked Example
Story: "A small island nation is brilliant at growing coffee but has no factories. A large neighbouring country makes excellent phones but has poor soil for coffee. If each tried to make BOTH coffee and phones alone, both would end up with less of each than they want. Instead, the island specialises in coffee, the neighbour specialises in phones, and they trade — now both countries enjoy great coffee AND great phones." Walk through how NEITHER country had to become good at the other's specialty to benefit.
Common Misconceptions
"Trade only helps the country that's best at making things." Fix: use the Trading Card Economy — BOTH teams gained more kinds of goods, even though neither team became better at anything new.
"Being able to do something faster means you should always do it yourself." Fix: use the Messi puzzle — what matters is what you GIVE UP (opportunity cost) by doing it yourself, not just raw speed.
"Free trade has no downsides for anyone." Fix: use the Advantages/Disadvantages tabs — local businesses that can't compete with cheaper imports can genuinely struggle.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
SpecialisationWhen a country focuses on producing what it makes best or cheapest.
Free tradeTrade between countries with no taxes or limits on what crosses the border.
Opportunity costWhat you give up by choosing to do one thing instead of another.
Gains from tradeThe extra goods and services everyone can enjoy when countries specialise and trade, instead of each trying to make everything alone.
