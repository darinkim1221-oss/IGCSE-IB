Detailed Teaching Notes
From calculation to critique
  • Hook: "if GDP rises, everyone in that country must be better off" — frame the lesson as testing this claim with calculations AND a structured critique, not just accepting or rejecting it by feel.
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Calculate / Comment before the calculator appears — "comment" demands a judgement about what the number does and doesn't tell us.
  • GDP calculator: drag the slider and watch the growth rate and per-capita figure recalculate together — insist students state the formula and substitution before reading off the final answer.
  • Real GDP per head vs. HDI tabs: HDI's three components (life expectancy, years of schooling, GNI per capita) are worth drilling by name — students often vaguely say "HDI includes health and education" without being able to name the third component.
  • Country comparison chart: the divergence between the gold and teal bars for a given country IS the lesson's key visual — push students to explain WHY a country might have a high income bar but a lower HDI bar (or the reverse).
  • Reasons for differences / IB bridge: keep "within a country" and "between countries" explanations distinct — students often blur them into one vague list. The circular-flow tab is explicitly IB Unit 3 scaffolding, not IGCSE content.
Visuals & Diagrams
📊
No formal D/S diagram is required for this lesson. The country comparison chart (paired GDP-per-capita and HDI bars) is the lesson's key VISUAL — a data comparison, not an economics model diagram. Do not require axes/curve labelling discipline here the way M1L2's D/S diagram does.
Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Discuss to what extent GDP per capita is a good measure of living standards." Model: (1) CLAIM — GDP per capita is a useful but incomplete measure; (2) EVIDENCE — it ignores income distribution, unpaid work, and environmental damage, while HDI adds health and education but still ignores within-country distribution; (3) LINK — conclude that GDP per capita works best as a quick, comparable snapshot, but should be read alongside HDI or distribution data for a fuller picture. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q5.
Common Misconceptions
"A country with a higher GDP per capita is automatically a better place to live." Fix: use the country comparison chart — a high income bar paired with a low HDI bar shows income isn't automatically translating into health or education outcomes.
"HDI is a perfect measure because it includes more factors than GDP." Fix: HDI still ignores income distribution within a country, and blending three measures into one score can hide which one is actually driving a change.
"GDP and GNI are the same thing." Fix: this lesson (and IGCSE 0455 2027–29) uses real GDP as the growth measure; GNI per capita appears ONLY as one of HDI's three components, not as a separate national-income measure to compare against GDP.

Exam-Precise Glossary
GDPThe total value of all goods and services produced within a country in a given year.
Real GDPGDP adjusted to strip out the effect of price changes (inflation), so genuine output change can be compared over time.
GDP per capitaReal GDP divided by population — a country's output shared out evenly per person.
HDIHuman Development Index — combines life expectancy, years of schooling, and GNI per capita into one score.
Living standardsHow good people's real, everyday quality of life is — broader than income alone.
