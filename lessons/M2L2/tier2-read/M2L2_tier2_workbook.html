5. Discuss to what extent GDP per capita is a good measure of a country's living standards. Structure your answer as CLAIM → EVIDENCE → LINK, referring to at least one limitation of GDP and to HDI as an alternative. [8]
Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. GDP is the total value of all goods and services produced within a country in a given year. Growth rate = (270−250)÷250×100 = 8%. [1 mark definition, 1 mark correct formula, 2 marks correct substitution and final answer]
2. GDP per capita = GDP ÷ population = 840,000,000,000 ÷ 12,000,000 = $70,000. [1 mark correct formula, 1 mark correct substitution, 1 mark correct final answer with unit]
3. Real GDP per head — Advantage: widely available, easily comparable single number. Disadvantage: ignores income distribution, unpaid work, and environmental cost. HDI — Advantage: captures health and education alongside income. Disadvantage: still ignores distribution within a country, and blends three measures into one number that can hide which is changing. [1 mark per correct, reasonable cell — 4 cells total]
4. Within a country: unequal access to education, healthcare, or income/wealth can leave some regions or groups far behind the national average. Between countries: differences in workforce productivity, natural resources, or investment in infrastructure and education can produce very different living standards even at similar income levels. [2 marks per correctly explained reason]

Band 3 (6–8 marks): a clear CLAIM (e.g. "GDP per capita is a useful but incomplete measure"), EVIDENCE (GDP per capita ignores income distribution, unpaid work, and environmental damage; HDI adds health and education but still has its own limits) and a LINK back to the question (a supported judgement such as "GDP per capita is most useful as a quick, comparable snapshot, but should be read alongside HDI or distribution data for a fuller picture"). Band 2 (3–5): correct limitations named but the claim-evidence-link chain is incomplete, or HDI is not used as a genuine comparison. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion with no named limitation or alternative indicator.
