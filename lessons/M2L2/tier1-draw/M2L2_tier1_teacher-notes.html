Detailed Teaching Notes
Count it, then question it
  • Hook: "which country is richer — more money, or better lives?" — let both sides argue before revealing anything.
  • Country in a Box: two mini-nations produce paper goods for three rounds. Boomtown's GDP pulls ahead each round — but its reveal each time (unfair profit-sharing, then river pollution) is what teaches GDP's real limitations. Let a student name the problem before you supply the vocabulary.
  • Happiness vs. income matching: four countries, each with an income level AND a life-satisfaction story that doesn't always move together — Country A shows high income doesn't guarantee high happiness, and Country D shows low income still hurts real lives. Avoid a flat "money doesn't matter" takeaway — it matters, just not perfectly.
  • Money-per-person vs. HDI: tap through three countries where the two scores diverge — this is the felt version of the exam-standard "GDP per head vs. HDI as living-standards indicators" comparison Tier 2 will formalise.
  • Fact-or-opinion game: consolidates GDP/HDI statements — a strong final check that students can separate what's testable from what's a value judgement.

Worked Example
Story: "An island has a GDP of $200 and 50 people living there. What is its GDP per person?" Walk through together: $200 ÷ 50 = $4 per person. Then ask: "does that $4 number tell us if the island shares its money fairly, or if its beaches are clean?" — the answer is no, which is exactly why GDP per person is useful but incomplete.
Common Misconceptions
"A country with a bigger GDP is always a better place to live." Fix: point to Boomtown's pollution and unfair profit-sharing — a high GDP can hide real problems.
"More money never makes anyone happier." Fix: use Country D from the happiness game — very low income is linked to real hardship and low life satisfaction, so money still matters, just not perfectly or forever.
"HDI and GDP per person always agree with each other." Fix: use the Country A/B comparison — a country can have high income but a lower HDI score, or the reverse.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
GDPThe total value of everything a country produces in one year.
GDP per personGDP shared out evenly across every person in the country.
HDIA score combining income, education, and healthy life expectancy into one number.
Living standardsHow good or comfortable people's everyday lives actually are.
