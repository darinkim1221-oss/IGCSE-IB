Detailed Teaching Notes
From trust to diagram to policy chain
  • Hook: "money only works because everyone trusts it will still be worth something tomorrow" — frame the lesson as testing what money actually needs, not memorising a definition.
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Explain / Compare before the full tabs appear — "compare" demands BOTH institutions' features, not one bank's story.
  • Money & banking tabs: four tabs — functions of money, characteristics of money, central bank, commercial bank. Keep functions (WHAT money does) and characteristics (properties it must HAVE) clearly separate — students often conflate the two lists.
  • Interest-rate slider: drag it and watch the incentive to save rise while the incentive to borrow falls — then name the other four influences (income, confidence, age, culture) as factors beyond the interest rate alone.
  • Labour market & minimum wage diagram: drill the sequence every time — draw D and S for labour, mark equilibrium, then add the minimum wage ABOVE equilibrium and label the resulting surplus (Qs > Qd) as unemployment. This diagram is newly explicit at IGCSE 2027–2029 — treat it with the same seriousness as the M1L4 intervention diagrams.
  • Labour market topics tabs: wage differences (skill/sector/discrimination/public-private/bargaining), division of labour (advantages/disadvantages of specialisation), and mobility of labour (occupational/geographical) — push students to apply each cause to a named, concrete job pair rather than reciting the list.
Visuals & Diagrams
📊
The labour-market diagram: demand and supply of labour crossing at an equilibrium wage, with a minimum wage line drawn ABOVE equilibrium and the resulting surplus (Qs − Qd) labelled explicitly. This is the lesson's one required diagram, newly explicit at IGCSE 2027–2029.
Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Discuss whether introducing a national minimum wage is good for workers." Model: (1) draw the labour-market diagram and mark the minimum wage above equilibrium; (2) identify that workers who keep their job now earn more (higher wage); (3) identify the COUNTER — firms demand less labour at the higher wage, so Qs > Qd, meaning some workers who would have been hired at the old equilibrium are not; (4) reach a judgement that depends on how far above equilibrium the wage is set, and how elastic labour demand is — this is the exact structure required in workbook Q5.
Common Misconceptions
"A bank just keeps your money safe until you want it back." Fix: point to the fractional-lending explanation — most of a deposit is lent out to earn interest, which is exactly why a "bank run" is a real risk.
"A minimum wage always makes workers better off." Fix: point to the surplus on the diagram — some workers earn more, but the resulting excess supply of labour means others may not be hired at all.
"Wage differences are only about how hard a job is." Fix: use the nurse-vs-cashier example — the relative SUPPLY of qualified workers (shaped by skill level, training, and bargaining strength) matters as much as how demanding the job feels.

Exam-Precise Glossary
MoneyAnything widely accepted as a medium of exchange, with the characteristics of durability, divisibility, portability, scarcity, acceptability and uniformity.
Central bankThe "bank for the banks" — issues currency, sets the base interest rate, acts as lender of last resort, and regulates the banking system. Not profit-driven.
Commercial bankA profit-driven bank that takes deposits from and lends to the public and businesses.
National minimum wage (NMW)A legal minimum wage set by government, typically above the free-market equilibrium wage — can cause a surplus of labour if set too high.
Division of labourBreaking production into separate tasks, each done by a specialised worker.
Mobility of labourHow easily a worker can move between jobs (occupational) or locations (geographical).
