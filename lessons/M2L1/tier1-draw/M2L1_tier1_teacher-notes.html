Detailed Teaching Notes
Discover, don't define, first
  • Hook: "could you get the whole class to accept bottle caps as money?" — let both sides argue before revealing anything.
  • Invent-your-own-currency game: students stress-test seashells, ice cubes, gold coins and bottle caps against six characteristics — the pattern (durable, divisible, portable, scarce, acceptable, uniform) should emerge from the failures, not be lectured first.
  • Mini-bank simulation: deposit → loan → "everyone wants it back at once" builds a gentle, felt sense of fractional reserve banking and why a bank run is scary, without ever naming "fractional reserve."
  • Salary auction: for each job, show how many people COULD do it and how much training it needs — let students predict the wage before revealing it. The big reveal (professional athlete) should land as a genuine surprise.
  • Family budget role-play: the same $200 windfall produces four different choices — draw out that income level, age, and confidence about the future all shape the decision, not just "how much money."
  • Fact-or-opinion game: consolidates money/bank/wage statements — a strong final check that students can separate what's testable from what's a value judgement.

Worked Example
Story: "A town has one baker who deposits $200 into the town's only bank. The bank keeps $40 in the vault and lends $160 to a farmer to buy seeds. A week later, three families all want to withdraw their savings on the same day — but the vault only has $40 in cash." Walk through why: the bank isn't being dishonest — most banks lend out most of what's deposited, because keeping 100% of it as idle cash would earn nothing. The risk only becomes real when too many people ask for cash back at the same time — this is exactly what the mini-bank simulation lets students discover for themselves.
Common Misconceptions
"A bank just keeps your money safe in a box until you want it back." Fix: point to the mini-bank simulation's loan step — most of a deposit is lent out to earn interest, which is exactly why a "bank run" is possible.
"People with harder jobs always earn more." Fix: use the salary auction — a professional athlete's job isn't necessarily "harder" than a nurse's, but far fewer people have that rare talent, which is what drives the wage up.
"Any object could become money if a group just decides it is." Fix: use the ice-cube and bottle-cap failures — an object still has to pass the durability and scarcity tests, or trust in it collapses fast.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
MoneyAnything widely accepted as payment — it must be trusted to hold its value.
BankA business that looks after deposits, pays interest on savings, and lends money out.
WageThe payment a worker receives for their labour.
Bank runWhen too many people try to withdraw their money from a bank at the same time.
Household budgetHow a family decides to spend, save, or borrow its income.
