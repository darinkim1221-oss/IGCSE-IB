Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel inequality and ageing before naming the theory
  • Hook: "if we shared 10 candies among 5 people, would everyone get 2?" — let students predict before revealing the real 6-2-1-1-0 split.
  • Why do rates vary? tabs: keep the causes concrete and comparative (healthcare, education, economic development, conflict) rather than naming specific countries, to avoid overgeneralising about any one nation.
  • Village of 100 People: four stages show the village ageing as birth rates fall and lifespans rise — end on the "who pays for pensions?" debate prompt without resolving it; this is meant to provoke discussion, not deliver an answer.
  • The Candy Economy: this is a DISCOVERY activity — let students react to the unequal split themselves before you name "inequality" as a concept. Do NOT introduce the formal term "Lorenz curve" at this tier; that comes at Tier 2 for IB-track students.
  • Absolute vs relative poverty: use a concrete example for each — absolute poverty as "can't afford food/water/shelter" anywhere in the world; relative poverty as "much less than the people around you," even in a wealthy country.
  • Policies to reduce poverty tabs: six policies, kept to one clear mechanism each — no need to compare their relative effectiveness at this tier (that's Tier 2 evaluation work).

Worked Example
Story: "A village has 100 people. In its early years, most families are young, with many children and few elderly residents. Over decades, healthcare improves and people live much longer — but families also choose to have fewer children. Fifty years later, the village has far more elderly residents than young workers, and the community must figure out how to support them." Walk through how BOTH a falling birth rate AND a falling death rate (people living longer) combine to age a population.
Common Misconceptions
"An equal split always feels equal to everyone." Fix: use the Candy Economy — even when 10 candies ARE "shared," the actual outcome (6-2-1-1-0) can feel very unfair to those who received little or nothing.
"Poverty only exists in poor countries." Fix: use the Absolute vs Relative Poverty tabs — relative poverty can exist in ANY country, including wealthy ones, if income is very unevenly shared.
"An ageing population is just about old people." Fix: use the Village simulation — it's a BALANCE between the number of young workers and elderly residents that creates the challenge, not just the number of elderly people alone.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
Birth rateThe number of babies born per 1,000 people each year.
Death rateThe number of deaths per 1,000 people each year.
Net migrationImmigration minus emigration.
Absolute povertyNot having enough to meet basic needs, anywhere in the world.
Relative povertyHaving much less than the average person in your own country.
