Extended Response · exam-style
6. Discuss whether progressive taxation and government benefits are an effective way to reduce income inequality. Structure your answer as CLAIM → EVIDENCE → LINK, referring to at least one specific limitation of redistribution policy. [8]
Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. Net migration = immigration − emigration. Here: 40,000 − 55,000 = −15,000 — negative net migration (more people left than arrived). [1 mark definition, 2 marks correct calculation and sign]
2. A falling birth rate narrows the BASE of the pyramid (fewer children born each year); rising life expectancy widens the TOP (more people surviving into old age) — over decades the pyramid shifts from a wide-based triangle toward a more rectangular or top-heavy shape. Consequence: a smaller working-age population must support a larger number of retirees, raising the tax burden per worker to fund pensions and healthcare. [2 marks correct shape-change explanation, 2 marks correct consequence]
3. Dependency ratio = (18 + 22) ÷ 60 × 100 = 40 ÷ 60 × 100 = 66.7%. This means for every 100 working-age people, there are roughly 67 dependents to support — a relatively high ratio that could strain public spending on pensions, healthcare and education relative to tax revenue collected from workers. [2 marks correct calculation, 2 marks reasonable comment]
4. Country Y (Gini = 0.58) has more income inequality — a higher Gini coefficient (closer to 1) means more inequality, a lower one (closer to 0) means more equality. Country X's Lorenz curve would sit CLOSE to the 45° diagonal (small gap = fairly equal); Country Y's Lorenz curve would bow much further AWAY from the diagonal (large gap = highly unequal). [1 mark correct country, 2 marks correct curve-shape description]
5. Cause (accept any reasonable, e.g.): low-skilled workers may earn wages that grow much more slowly than the national average, leaving them increasingly poor relative to others even if their absolute income doesn't fall. Policy (accept any reasonable, e.g.): raising the minimum wage directly increases the income floor for the lowest-paid workers, narrowing the gap with average income — though this must be explained with its mechanism, not just named. [2 marks per correctly explained point]
6. Band 3 (6–8 marks): CLAIM (e.g. "redistribution can meaningfully reduce inequality but has real limits"); EVIDENCE (progressive taxation takes a larger share of income from high earners, and transfer payments/benefits directly raise the income of low earners — together these narrow the gap between the top and bottom of the income distribution, as shown by a Lorenz curve moving closer to the diagonal; however, very high tax rates can reduce the incentive to work or invest, and some benefits can create a "poverty trap" where earning more causes a similarly-sized loss of benefits); LINK (a supported conclusion such as "redistribution is effective at reducing the SYMPTOMS of inequality but works best alongside policies — like education and training — that address its underlying causes"). Band 2 (3–5): explains redistribution's effect but no clearly explained limitation. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion with no mechanism.
