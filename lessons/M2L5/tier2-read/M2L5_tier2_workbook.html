Extended Response · exam-style
6. Evaluate whether monetary policy or fiscal policy is more effective at reducing unemployment. Structure your answer with a strength and a limitation for EACH policy, then a context-dependent judgement. [8]
Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. Fiscal policy is the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy. Monetary policy is the use of tools like interest rates to influence money supply and spending. The key difference: fiscal policy is controlled by the government (spending/tax); monetary policy is typically controlled by a central bank (interest rates/money supply). [2 marks per correct definition]
2. Pushing unemployment very low means businesses compete for a scarce supply of workers and raise wages to attract them; these higher wage costs tend to be passed on as higher prices, conflicting with the aim of stable prices. [3 marks for a complete causal chain]
3. Budget deficit = spending − tax revenue = $120bn − $105bn = $15bn deficit. [1 mark formula, 1 mark substitution, 1 mark correct labelled answer]
4. (a) Progressive, direct. (b) Proportional, direct. (c) Neither progressive nor regressive by definition alone (accept "regressive in effect" with reasoning), indirect. [1 mark per correct classification, up to 6]
5. E.g. education & training raises worker productivity, letting the same workforce produce more; infrastructure spending lowers business costs and raises the economy's capacity to produce over time. Any two named, correctly explained measures accepted (labour market reforms, lower direct taxes, deregulation, incentives, privatisation). [3 marks per correctly explained measure]
6. Band 3 (6–8 marks): Monetary policy strength (fast to implement, e.g. central banks can cut rates quickly) and limitation (relies on businesses/consumers actually being willing to borrow and spend — can be ineffective if confidence is very low). Fiscal policy strength (government spending can create jobs directly, e.g. public infrastructure projects) and limitation (slower to implement due to budget/political processes, and can worsen the budget deficit). Judgement: monetary policy may work faster in a mild slowdown, while fiscal policy may be more effective in a deep recession where confidence is too low for rate cuts to have much effect. Band 2 (3–5): a strength and limitation for each policy without a clear judgement. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion favouring one policy with no named limitation.
