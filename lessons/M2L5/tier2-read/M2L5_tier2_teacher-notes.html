Detailed Teaching Notes
Full syllabus rigor — three toolkits, one evaluation skill
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Calculate / Comment first — the calculate item previews the budget calculator that follows immediately after.
  • Macro aims & conflicts: nine tabs in one slide (six aims, three conflicts, visually flagged with a ⚡ icon and red active state) — treat the conflicts as the payoff of knowing the aims, not a separate topic.
  • Budget calculator: drill the sequence every time — spending is fixed, revenue moves via the slider, and the LABEL (deficit/surplus/balanced) must match the sign of the result, not just the number.
  • Tax classifications: stress these are TWO independent classifications — progressive/regressive/proportional is about WHO pays what percentage; direct/indirect is about HOW the tax is collected. A tax can be both, e.g. progressive AND direct.
  • Monetary policy: the exchange-rate channel is explicitly a NAME-ONLY item at IGCSE — don't let students try to model currency markets in detail here; that depth is IB-only.
  • Supply-side policy: eight tabs (definition + seven measures) — push students to explain the MECHANISM ("raises productivity" / "lowers business costs"), not just list the measure's name.
  • IB HL enrichment: flag clearly that sustainable government debt and the Keynesian multiplier are NOT required at IGCSE 0455 — these bridge directly into IB HL macro.

Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Evaluate whether monetary policy or fiscal policy is more effective at reducing unemployment." Model: STRENGTH (monetary) — a central bank can cut interest rates quickly, encouraging borrowing and spending. LIMITATION (monetary) — this only works if businesses and consumers are actually willing to borrow; in a severe downturn, confidence may be too low. STRENGTH (fiscal) — government spending can create jobs directly (e.g. infrastructure projects). LIMITATION (fiscal) — slower to implement due to budget/political processes, and can worsen the budget deficit. JUDGEMENT — monetary policy may act faster in a mild slowdown, while fiscal policy may be more effective in a deep recession where rate cuts alone won't restore confidence. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q6.
Common Misconceptions
"A budget deficit means the country is broke." Fix: use the budget calculator — a deficit simply means spending exceeds revenue in ONE year; it adds to accumulated government debt, but a moderate, temporary deficit funding productive investment is not automatically harmful.
"Progressive and direct are the same classification." Fix: use the Tax Classifications tabs — progressive/regressive/proportional describes the RATE structure; direct/indirect describes HOW the tax is collected. They are independent.
"Supply-side policy works as fast as fiscal or monetary policy." Fix: stress explicitly that supply-side measures (education, infrastructure) take YEARS to raise productive capacity, unlike an interest-rate cut or a spending announcement.

Exam-Precise Glossary
Fiscal policyThe use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
Budget deficit / surplusWhen government spending exceeds tax revenue (deficit) or is less than tax revenue (surplus).
Monetary policyThe use of tools like interest rates to influence money supply and spending.
Supply-side policyMeasures that increase the economy's productive capacity.
Progressive taxTakes a bigger percentage from higher earners.
Regressive taxTakes a smaller percentage from higher earners.
Direct / indirect taxPaid directly by the earner (direct) vs. added to the price of a good or service (indirect).
