Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel the levers and their trade-offs — then name them
  • Hook: "if you could only pull ONE lever" — let students argue for spending vs. interest rates before revealing there's a third, slower lever too (supply-side).
  • You Are the Central Bank: the dial starts at 50 ("balanced"). Students react to news headlines by raising, lowering, or holding rates — let a student predict the effect BEFORE revealing the note.
  • Run the National Budget: five tabs — two on WHY governments spend/tax, three on tax TYPES (proportional, progressive, indirect). Keep tax classification brief and concrete — this is a "meet the words," not a mastery target, at this tier.
  • Every Fix Creates a New Problem: three rounds, each showing a "fix" then the new problem it creates — this is the felt version of the three named macro conflicts (full employment vs. stable prices; growth vs. environment; full employment vs. balance of payments).
  • Supply-side toolkit: keep to the four core measures (education/training, infrastructure, labour market reforms, lower direct taxes) — frame this explicitly as the SLOWER, longer-term lever compared to fiscal/monetary policy.
  • Fact-or-opinion game: a strong final check that students can separate a testable claim (e.g. "a central bank can raise interest rates") from a value judgement (e.g. "the government should always cut taxes").

Worked Example
Story: "Prices have been rising fast across the country. The central bank raises interest rates. Borrowing a loan for a new car or a house now costs more, so families and businesses put off big purchases. Total spending slows down — and so does the pace of rising prices. But some businesses that were hoping to expand now hold back, and a few workers who would have been hired aren't." Walk through how ONE decision (raising rates) has both an intended effect (slower price rises) and a side effect (slower job creation).
Common Misconceptions
"The government and the central bank are the same thing." Fix: use the cover slide's two characters — the government (fiscal policy: spending & tax) and the central bank (monetary policy: interest rates) are different institutions with different tools.
"Progressive tax means everyone pays more." Fix: use the Budget game's tax tabs — progressive means a BIGGER PERCENTAGE for higher earners, not a bigger amount for everyone.
"Fixing unemployment or inflation has no downsides." Fix: use the Every Fix Creates a New Problem game — every one of the three classic conflicts shows a real trade-off, not a free win.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
Macroeconomic aimA goal a government wants for the whole economy.
Fiscal policyUsing government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
Monetary policyUsing tools like interest rates to influence the economy.
Supply-side policyPolicies that help the economy produce more, like better education or infrastructure.
Progressive taxA tax that takes a bigger percentage from higher earners.
Proportional taxA tax that takes the same percentage from everyone.
