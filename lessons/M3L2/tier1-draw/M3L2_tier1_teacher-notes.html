Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel the ripple before naming the tools
  • Hook: "what happens when a country builds a wall around its market?" — let both sides argue before revealing the tariff round.
  • Tariff Round: this directly continues M3L1's Trading Card Economy — students should already know both teams reached 4 kinds of goods each under free trade. Adding a tariff drops BOTH teams back down, making the cost of protection visible and immediate.
  • The People vs. The Tariff: four voices — consumer, domestic worker, government, foreign producer — each with a genuine, sympathetic point of view. Resist framing any one voice as simply "right" — the whole point is that a tariff creates real winners AND real losers at once.
  • Why restrict trade?: five tabs matching the exact syllabus-listed reasons (infant industries, declining industries, strategic industries, demerit goods, environmental sustainability) — deliberately excludes anti-dumping, reducing a deficit, and raising tax revenue, which are Tier 2 content.
  • Winners & losers sort: a clean recap that consolidates the courtroom voices into a simple two-bucket sort — good formative check before the quiz.

Worked Example
Story: "A country's shoe factories are struggling because imported shoes are much cheaper. The government adds a tariff on imported shoes. Now local shoe shops sell more, and factory workers keep their jobs — but every shopper in the country now pays a bit more for shoes, whether local or imported." Walk through how ONE policy (the tariff) helps SOME people (domestic workers) while it costs OTHERS (consumers) — there's no single "winner" for the whole country.
Common Misconceptions
"A tariff only affects foreign producers." Fix: use the courtroom voices — consumers pay more too, since tariffs raise prices on imported goods that shoppers buy.
"Trade restrictions have no real reason behind them." Fix: use the Why Restrict Trade? tabs — governments give specific, real reasons (protecting new industries, national security, health, environment).
"A tariff and a subsidy do the same thing." Fix: a tariff TAXES foreign goods; a subsidy PAYS domestic producers directly. Both can help local businesses, but through opposite mechanisms.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
TariffA tax placed on imported goods.
Import quotaA limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported.
SubsidyA payment from the government to domestic producers.
Infant industryA brand-new local industry that may need temporary protection to grow strong.
Strategic industryAn industry (like food or energy) a country doesn't want to depend on others for.
