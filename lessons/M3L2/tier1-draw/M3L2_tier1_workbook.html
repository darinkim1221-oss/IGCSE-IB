Warm-up
Show what you discovered today
1. What is a tariff? Circle the best answer.
  • A limit on how much of a good can be imported
  • A tax placed on imported goods
  • A payment the government gives to foreign sellers
2. Fill in the blank: a limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported is called an import ______________.
3. Match each courtroom voice to what they'd likely say about a tariff.
"Now people buy from us again!"
"I have to pay more for my sneakers now."
"We're selling far fewer shoes here now."
Choices: Domestic Worker · Consumer · Foreign Producer
4. In the Trading Card Economy, what happened to BOTH teams' total goods when a tariff was added? Explain in 1–2 sentences.
5. Name ONE reason a government might restrict trade, and give an example.
  • Infant (sunrise) industries
  • Declining (sunset) industries
  • Strategic industries
  • Restricting demerit goods
  • Environmental sustainability
6. Name ONE winner and ONE loser from a tariff, and explain why for each.
7. What is a subsidy, and how is it different from a tariff?
8. If you were in charge, would you use a tariff to protect a local industry? Give ONE reason for your answer.
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Have you ever noticed a product get more expensive because of taxes on imports? What was it?" Write down what they remember.
Answer Key
1. "A tax placed on imported goods." — Tariffs make imports more expensive by taxing them.
2. Quota. — An import quota limits quantity, not price directly.
3. "Now people buy from us again": Domestic Worker. "I have to pay more": Consumer. "We're selling far fewer shoes": Foreign Producer.
4. Both teams' total goods FELL — the tariff made trading more expensive, so both teams traded less with each other than they did under free trade.
5. Accept any one of the five reasons with a matching example (e.g. "Infant industries — protecting a brand-new local chip factory until it can compete with huge foreign companies").
6. Winner: domestic producers (less competition from imports, so they sell more) OR government (collects tariff revenue). Loser: consumers (higher prices) OR foreign producers (sell less). Accept any correctly reasoned pair.
7. A subsidy is a payment FROM the government TO domestic producers, helping them compete — unlike a tariff, which is a tax ON imports. Both aim to help local producers, but a subsidy directly supports them rather than taxing foreign competitors.
8. Accept any reasoned answer that names a real trade-off (e.g. "Yes, to protect jobs, even though prices might rise a little" or "No, because it would make things more expensive for everyone").
