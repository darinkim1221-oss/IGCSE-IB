Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. A tariff is a tax placed on imported goods, raising their price. An import quota is a limit on the QUANTITY of a good that can be imported, regardless of price. The key difference: a tariff works through PRICE; a quota works through a fixed QUANTITY limit. [2 marks per correct definition]
2. (a) Promoting environmental sustainability. (b) Raising tax revenue. (c) Strategic industries. [1 mark per correct identification]
3. Downward-sloping demand, upward-sloping supply crossing at domestic equilibrium ABOVE the world price line; at Pw, the horizontal gap between the demand curve and supply curve (Qd − Qs at that price) is the import gap, clearly labelled. [2 marks correct D/S shapes and equilibrium, 2 marks world price line and import gap correctly identified and labelled]
4. Domestic supply RISES (producers respond to the higher price by supplying more — a movement along the supply curve). Domestic demand FALLS (consumers respond to the higher price by buying less — a movement along the demand curve). The import quantity SHRINKS, since it equals the new smaller gap between demand and supply at the higher price. [4 marks: ~1.5 per correctly explained direction, all three parts needed for full marks]
5. The four areas: consumer surplus loss, producer surplus gain, government tariff revenue, and deadweight loss. Deadweight loss represents the loss of overall economic efficiency — it is not captured as a gain by any group (consumers, producers, or government), unlike the other three areas. [1 mark per correctly named area, up to 4]
6. Band 3 (6–8 marks): CLAIM (e.g. "a tariff can be justified as a short-term measure but carries real costs"); EVIDENCE (winners: domestic workers in the declining industry keep their jobs longer, and the government may gain tariff revenue; losers: consumers face higher prices and reduced choice, and trading partners lose export sales and may retaliate); LINK (a supported conclusion such as "a temporary, well-targeted tariff giving the industry time to restructure may be justified, but a permanent tariff that never lets the industry adapt likely costs consumers more than it's worth long-term"). Band 2 (3–5): names a winner and a loser but no clear final link/judgement. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion with no named opposing consideration.
