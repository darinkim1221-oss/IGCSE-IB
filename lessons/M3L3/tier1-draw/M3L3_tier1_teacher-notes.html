Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel the currency move before naming the mechanics
  • Hook: "what decides how much your money is worth in another country?" — most students assume money has a fixed value everywhere; let this surprise them.
  • Currency Exchange Booth: four news events (K-pop tour, tourists staying home, remittances, nervous investors) each nudge a shared "currency value" bar up or down — this is the felt version of "causes of fluctuations" without the formal demand/supply-of-currency diagram (that's Tier 2).
  • Why buy foreign money? tabs: all six syllabus-listed reasons, explained in plain, concrete language — trade, speculation, government intervention, profit/interest/dividends, remittances, and investment abroad.
  • Travel Budget Challenge: the SAME $1,000 trip costs more home-currency after the currency weakens — this is the felt version of "consequences for prices of exports/imports," using something every student can relate to (a trip).
  • Current account tabs: keep the four components concrete with one example each; keep deficit/surplus as simple as "more money in than out = surplus, more out than in = deficit" — no calculations at this tier.
Visuals & Diagrams
📊
No formal currency-market diagram is introduced at this tier. The Currency Exchange Booth's value bar is a felt, non-formal preview — the formal demand-and-supply-of-a-currency diagram, appreciation/depreciation vocabulary, and equilibrium exchange rate are explicitly Tier 2 content.
Worked Example
Story: "A country's biggest pop group announces a world tour. Fans overseas rush to buy concert tickets and merchandise, which means they need to exchange their own money for this country's currency. All that extra demand makes the currency stronger. A family planning a trip TO another country now finds their OWN money buys even more abroad than it used to." Walk through how a single real-world event (the tour) changes the currency's value, which then changes what a family's money is worth on a trip.
Common Misconceptions
"Money is worth the same amount everywhere." Fix: use the Currency Exchange Booth — a currency's value shifts constantly based on real-world events.
"A weaker currency is always bad news." Fix: note (without over-teaching) that a weaker currency makes a country's own exports cheaper for foreigners to buy, even though it makes foreign trips/imports more expensive for locals — there are two sides.
"The current account is about company profits, not the whole country." Fix: use the Current Account tabs — it tracks money flows for the WHOLE country, including trade, income, and remittances.

Kid-Friendly Glossary
Foreign exchange rateThe price of one country's money in terms of another's.
RemittancesMoney workers abroad send home to their families.
Current accountA record of money flowing in and out of a country from trade and other international income.
DeficitMore money going out than coming in.
SurplusMore money coming in than going out.
