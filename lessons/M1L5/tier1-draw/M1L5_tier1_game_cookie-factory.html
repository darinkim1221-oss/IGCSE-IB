The Cookie Factory: Grow, Merge, or Crash?
M1L5 · Tier 1 game — hire bakers round by round, watch your average cost per cookie fall... then rise. Halfway through, a rival factory knocks with a merger offer. Grow smart, not just big.

🧠 For Tutors — Game Guide & Debrief
What this game teaches
Economies and diseconomies of scale, experienced as a felt curve rather than a memorised diagram. Hiring more bakers spreads the factory's fixed costs (the oven, the rent) over more cookies, so average cost per cookie falls at first — that's economies of scale. But past a certain workforce size, the factory floor gets crowded, bakers get in each other's way, and average cost starts rising again — diseconomies of scale. Partway through, a rival factory offers a horizontal merger, forcing the student to apply the same "is bigger actually better right now?" judgement to a real firm decision, not just a hiring slider.
Core mechanic
Each of 6 rounds, the student chooses an action that changes the factory's baker count: Hire 1, Hire 3, Buy a Machine (+2, counted as extra capacity), or Hold Steady (+0, bank the round safely). The game computes average cost per cookie from a U-shaped formula (cost falls as bakers rise from 1 toward roughly 5, then climbs again past roughly 8–9 as the floor gets crowded), multiplies by output to get the round's profit, and adds it to a running total. After round 3, a rival factory ("Rival Cookie Co.") interrupts with a merger offer — accepting instantly adds their workforce to the player's factory, which can either push the player right into the efficient zone or straight into an overcrowded, loss-making one, depending on how big the player already is.
Round structure
  • Briefing: the factory starts with 1 baker; the student is told costs will fall, then eventually rise, as the workforce grows.
  • Rounds 1–3: student picks an action each round, sees bakers, average cost, output, and round profit immediately.
  • Merger event (after round 3): Rival Cookie Co., with its own workforce, offers to merge. Accept (horizontal merger — combined workforce, one factory) or stay independent.
  • Rounds 4–6: the same hiring choices continue, now from whatever workforce size the merger decision produced.
  • Result: total profit across all 6 rounds, final workforce size, and which zone of the cost curve the factory ended in.
Pre-game brief (1 minute)
"You run a cookie factory. Right now you have just 1 baker, and cookies are expensive to make because the oven and the rent are being paid for by almost nobody. Hire more bakers and each cookie gets cheaper — for a while. But cram too many bakers onto one factory floor and they start tripping over each other, and cost creeps back up. Watch the numbers, not just your instinct to keep growing."
Debrief questions
  • At what baker count was your average cost per cookie the lowest? What happened to it after that point, and why?
  • When Rival Cookie Co. offered to merge, did you accept or reject — and looking at your workforce size at that moment, was that the right call?
  • Is "hire as many bakers as possible" ever the best strategy in this game? What does that tell you about how real factories decide how big to grow?
Common misconceptions & fix
"Bigger is always cheaper per unit." Have the student compare their average cost at 5 bakers versus 11–12 bakers on their own log — the number visibly climbs back up, which is diseconomies of scale, not a glitch. "A merger is automatically good because the factory gets bigger." Point out that accepting the merger when the factory is already near its efficient size can push it straight into the crowded, high-cost zone — size only helps up to a point, and a merger is just another way to change size fast.
Variants
Simpler: remove the merger event entirely and just play 6 rounds of hiring, focusing purely on finding the lowest-average-cost workforce size. Harder: after finishing, ask the student to sketch their own average-cost-vs-bakers curve on paper from their round-by-round log, then compare it to the class's cost-curve diagram — a direct bridge into the Tier 2 ATC diagram.
