🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Have you ever noticed prices rising on something you buy regularly? What was it, and how did it change what you bought?" Write down their example.
Answer Key
1. "People buying more stuff." — A rise in total demand is one of the three main causes of economic growth.
2. Inflation. — Rising prices mean the same amount of money buys less over time.
3. Losing regular pay: Person. Paying out more benefits: Government. Fewer customers buying goods: Business.
4. Too many people wanted the candy bar at once, but shops couldn't make enough to meet that demand — when demand is bigger than supply, the price gets pulled up. This is called demand-pull inflation.
5. Accept two bars with Round 3 taller than Round 1 (e.g. $1 vs $3) — the key skill is drawing/reading a simple bar graph showing a rise.
6. Accept any reasoned answer — check the reason actually matches the choice (e.g. "Training, because it gives people the skills businesses actually need" or "Government spending, because it creates jobs right away").
7. A recession is when total spending in the economy falls and the economy shrinks instead of growing — factories produce less and some workers may lose their jobs.
8. Inflation HURTS savers — the money they saved buys less later than when they saved it. Inflation can HELP borrowers — they pay back their loan with money that is worth less than when they borrowed it.
