Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. Unemployment rate = (number unemployed ÷ labour force) × 100 = (1,500,000 ÷ 25,000,000) × 100 = 6%. [1 mark formula, 1 mark substitution, 1 mark correct labelled answer]
2. Inflation rate = (108 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = 8%. This would hurt savers and people on fixed incomes the most, since their money buys noticeably less as prices rise 8% while their income/savings value stays the same. [2 marks calculation, 2 marks valid named group + reasoning]
3. (a) Seasonal — tied to a predictable time of year (tourist season). (b) Structural — a long-term change in the economy's structure requiring retraining. (c) Cyclical — tied to a downturn across the whole economy (the economic cycle). [2 marks per correct type + valid reasoning]
4. E.g. a rise in total demand (more household/government/export spending) can pull real GDP up; separately, an increase in the quantity of resources (a larger workforce or more machinery) or quality of resources (better training raising productivity) can raise what the economy is capable of producing. [2 marks per correctly explained, distinct cause]
5. Consumers: job insecurity and falling confidence, so many cut back spending further. Firms: falling sales and profits, often leading to cuts in investment or staff. Government: falls in tax revenue (less income/spending to tax) combined with a rise in unemployment-support spending, worsening the government's budget position. [2 marks per group, each needing a specific consequence, not just "it gets worse"]
6. Band 3 (6–8 marks): a clear CLAIM (e.g. "unemployment is generally the more serious problem because it removes income entirely rather than eroding its value"), EVIDENCE naming a specific affected group for each problem (an unemployed worker loses their whole income and can face lasting skill loss; a saver under inflation loses only some purchasing power and some groups such as borrowers can even benefit), and a LINK back to the question acknowledging the answer can depend on severity (very high inflation, e.g. hyperinflation, could be judged more damaging than mild unemployment). Band 2 (3–5): a claim with some relevant evidence but no explicit link/judgement. Band 1 (1–2): a one-sided assertion with no named affected group.
