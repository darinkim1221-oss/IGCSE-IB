Detailed Teaching Notes
Full syllabus rigor — calculation, classification, evaluation
  • Command-term drill: run Define / Calculate / Comment before the calculator slides — this primes students that "calculate and comment" questions award marks for the working AND the comment separately.
  • Unemployment rate calculator: drill the sequence every time — formula, substitution, labelled answer. The slider lets students see the rate respond in real time as the numerator changes while the labour force (denominator) stays fixed.
  • CPI & inflation rate calculator: stress that CPI itself is an INDEX (base year = 100, not a currency value) — a common error is treating CPI values as prices.
  • Types of unemployment: this is new-explicit content at IGCSE 2027-29 — four NAMED types are now examinable (frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal), not just "unemployment" as a single idea. Push students to identify the type from a scenario, not just recite the definition.
  • Growth & recession tabs: the three causes of growth (total demand, quantity of resources, quality of resources) are the same three factors that, in REVERSE, cause recession — draw this symmetry out explicitly.
  • IB HL enrichment: flag clearly, every time, that deflation/deflationary-spiral, PPC/LRAS growth framing, and the Phillips curve are NOT required at IGCSE 0455. These bridge directly into IB HL macro.

Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "Discuss whether unemployment or inflation is the more serious problem for an economy to solve." Model: CLAIM — unemployment is generally more serious because it removes a person's income entirely, rather than eroding its value. EVIDENCE — an unemployed worker loses their whole income and may face lasting skill loss (structural unemployment), while inflation under a saver only erodes some purchasing power, and some groups (e.g. borrowers repaying fixed-rate debt) can even gain. LINK — conclude the answer can depend on severity: very high inflation (hyperinflation) could be judged more damaging than a mild, short-term rise in unemployment. This is the exact structure required in workbook Q6.
Common Misconceptions
"CPI is a price, so a CPI of 108 means something costs $108." Fix: stress CPI is an INDEX relative to a base year of 100 — the inflation rate calculator only uses the percentage CHANGE between two CPI values, never CPI as a currency figure.
"All unemployment is the same problem." Fix: use the Types of Unemployment tabs — frictional and seasonal unemployment are largely unavoidable even in a healthy economy, while structural and cyclical unemployment are the ones policy usually targets.
"Growth has no downsides." Fix: use the Growth & Recession "Advantages & Disadvantages" tab — growth that outpaces resource growth can cause demand-pull inflation, widen inequality, or increase environmental strain.

Exam-Precise Glossary
Unemployment rate(Number unemployed ÷ labour force) × 100.
Consumer Prices Index (CPI)An index tracking the cost of a fixed "basket" of goods and services relative to a base year (=100).
Inflation rate(This year's CPI − last year's CPI) ÷ last year's CPI × 100.
Frictional unemploymentShort-term unemployment while between jobs.
Structural unemploymentCaused by a long-term change in the economy's structure, requiring retraining.
Cyclical unemploymentCaused by a downturn in the economic cycle (a recession).
Seasonal unemploymentFollows a predictable seasonal pattern (e.g. tourism, agriculture).
RecessionA period where real GDP falls, caused by a fall in total demand or in the quantity/quality of resources.
