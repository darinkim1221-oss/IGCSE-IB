Warm-up
Show what you discovered today
1. In the Pit Market game, when does a trade happen? Circle the best answer.
  • Only when the teacher says so.
  • When the buyer's top price is at least the seller's lowest price.
  • Whenever two people shake hands.
2. Fill in the blank: When the price of something drops, ______________ people usually want to buy it.
3. A new sneaker has 500 buyers but only 50 pairs. Is this a shortage or a surplus? Explain in one sentence.
4. Name TWO things (besides price) that could make you want to buy MORE of something.
5. True or False: If a shop has way more stock than buyers want, the price usually needs to rise to fix it.
6. In Market Rush, what happened to the QUEUE every time you raised the price?
7. In Market Rush, what happened to the SHELF (stock) every time you raised the price?
8. A factory finds a cheaper way to make shoes. Would sellers want to sell MORE or LESS at the same price? Why?
9. What is a "nudge"? Give an example from the lesson or your own life.
10. Nobody is "in charge" of prices, yet they still change. In your own words, explain how a price "knows" when to move.
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Have you ever noticed a price change because something was in short supply, or because too much was left unsold?" Write down their example and whether it was a shortage or a surplus.
Answer Key
1. "When the buyer's top price is at least the seller's lowest price." — That overlap is where a deal becomes possible.
2. More. — This is the Law of Demand: lower price, more buyers willing and able to buy.
3. Shortage. Accept: "There are far more buyers than pairs available at that price."
4. Accept any two: it becomes trendy, friends have it, you get more money (allowance/birthday), the season changes, an influencer promotes it, etc.
5. False. — A surplus (too much stock, not enough buyers) usually means the price needs to FALL to attract more buyers and clear the extra stock.
6. The queue shrank — fewer buyers were willing to pay the higher price.
7. The shelf grew — sellers were willing to offer more stock at the higher price.
8. MORE — cheaper production costs mean sellers can make a profit even while selling at the same price, so they're willing to supply more.
9. A nudge is a small design choice that encourages a certain choice without banning anything else — e.g. putting healthy snacks at eye level. Accept any reasonable real-life example.
10. Sample: "When too many people want to buy at a price, buyers offer more and the price rises; when too much is left unsold, sellers accept less and the price falls. No one plans it — it happens because everyone reacts to what they see (queues, empty shelves, unsold stock)." Accept any answer capturing this self-adjusting idea.
