Elasticity is the course's first fully quantitative analytical tool, and it is the most heavily calculation-tested topic in Module 1 for both boards. Where M1L2 built diagram fluency, M1L3 builds calculation fluency — the discipline of showing every step, not just stating a final number. Students who master the three-line "formula → substitution → answer + comment" habit here will reuse it directly in cost/revenue calculations (M1L5) and in any numerical IB Paper 3 question. Treat the calculation technique itself, not just the economics, as the core deliverable of this lesson.
Detailed Teaching Notes
From hypothesis to proof
Open with the hook framed as a testable hypothesis (slide s0): "raising a price can sometimes make a firm LESS money — and elasticity tells you exactly when." This reframes elasticity as a tool that resolves a genuine puzzle, not an abstract vocabulary list. Run the command-term drill (slide s1) on PED across Define / State / Describe before any numbers appear, so students separate "knowing what it means" from "knowing how to calculate it."
The calculation walkthrough (slide s2, train ticket $10→$12, quantity 100→70) is the load-bearing skill of this lesson. Drill the exact sequence every time, with no shortcuts: (1) %ΔQd = change ÷ original × 100; (2) %ΔP = change ÷ original × 100; (3) PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP; (4) state the value; (5) interpret the value against the five-value vocabulary; (6) state the implication (here, PED = −1.5 → elastic → a price rise would REDUCE revenue). Insist students write all six steps even when they can do the arithmetic in their heads — this is exactly the AO2 "show all working" structure IGCSE markers reward with method marks even if the final answer is wrong.
The five-value vocabulary tour (slide s3) should be taught as a spectrum, not five disconnected boxes: perfectly inelastic (vertical curve, |PED|=0) → inelastic (0<|PED|<1) → unitary (|PED|=1) → elastic (|PED|>1) → perfectly elastic (horizontal curve, |PED|=∞). For each, require students to state BOTH the curve shape AND a plausible real-world example — examiners commonly ask for one or the other in isolation.
The PED–revenue link (slide s4) is the single most exam-relevant idea in the lesson. Use the elastic/inelastic toggle to show the shaded "old revenue" rectangle vs the outlined "new revenue" rectangle for the SAME price rise applied to goods with different elasticities — this makes visually obvious why the identical percentage price rise can either raise or lower revenue depending only on PED. State the general rule explicitly and have students repeat it back: raise price on an INELASTIC good → revenue rises; raise price on an ELASTIC good → revenue falls; at UNITARY elasticity, revenue is unchanged by a small price change.
Determinants of PED (slide s5) should be taught as a memorised, exam-ready list of six: availability of substitutes, necessity vs luxury, proportion of income spent, time period, habit/addiction, breadth of definition of the good. Push students to apply — not just recite — these by testing them on unfamiliar goods in the moment.
PES (slide s6) mirrors PED's structure exactly (formula, worked calculation, five-value interpretation, determinants), which is worth stating explicitly to reduce cognitive load — "everything you just learned about demand has a supply-side twin." PES determinants: time available to increase production, spare capacity, ability to store output, and factor mobility (how easily inputs can be redirected into producing more).
IB enrichment (slide s7) covers YED (income elasticity — sign, not just size, matters: positive = normal good, negative = inferior good) and tax incidence (IB HL only — inelastic demand means buyers absorb more of a tax). Flag both explicitly as beyond IGCSE 0455. Note for IB-track teachers: the full incidence-SPLIT calculation (exact $ burden on each side) is HL-level; SL students and all IGCSE students only need the qualitative direction of the argument (inelastic demand → sellers can pass on more of the tax).
Visuals & Diagrams
📉
Five-value curve family: perfectly inelastic is a vertical line; perfectly elastic is a horizontal line; the other three are downward-sloping lines of increasing flatness from inelastic (steep) through unitary (moderate) to elastic (shallow). Drawing all five on axes with the same scale side by side makes the spectrum concrete rather than abstract.
🟩
Revenue rectangles: total revenue = price × quantity is always the area of a rectangle under the equilibrium point. Shade the ORIGINAL revenue rectangle and outline the NEW revenue rectangle after a price change on the same axes — students should be able to see by eye whether the new rectangle's area is bigger or smaller than the old one, before doing any arithmetic.
Tip: when a student states a PED value with the wrong sign or forgets it should usually be negative, don't just correct the sign — ask them to check whether price and quantity moved in the same or opposite directions on the demand curve, and let them re-derive the sign.
Worked Example
Exam-style prompt: "A market stall raises the price of scarves from $10 to $13. Quantity demanded falls from 50 to 30 per week. Calculate PED and explain the effect on revenue." Model the full six-step chain: (1) %ΔQd = (30−50)/50 × 100 = −40%; (2) %ΔP = (13−10)/10 × 100 = +30%; (3) PED = −40 ÷ 30 = −1.33; (4) state the value; (5) |PED| > 1 → elastic; (6) implication — because demand is elastic, the proportional fall in quantity (40%) outweighs the proportional rise in price (30%), so total revenue FALLS (from $500 to $390). This is the exact structure used in workbook Q6 — walk it on the board before students attempt it independently.
Common Misconceptions
"A bigger PED number always means a 'better' or 'more important' good." Fix: PED size measures RESPONSIVENESS to price, not importance or value — insulin is enormously important but very inelastic (|PED| close to 0) precisely because buyers have no choice but to keep buying it regardless of price.
"Raising price always raises revenue" / "cutting price always raises revenue." Fix: both are half-true at best — the direction depends entirely on PED. Drill the rule as a conditional statement, never as a universal one: IF demand is inelastic, a price RISE raises revenue; IF demand is elastic, a price RISE cuts revenue (and vice versa for a price cut).
"PES works exactly like PED, so I can skip re-deriving it." Fix: the formula and five-value logic are structurally identical, but the determinants are different (time to produce, spare capacity, storability, factor mobility — not substitutes/necessity/budget share). Require students to state PES's own determinant list, not PED's, when asked.
Exam Adapter Slot
Source: Wiki §7 drip map, row M1 L3 ("Analysis")
Skill focus this lesson: PED/PES/YED calculations with full working; interpret the value; state the implication. Command terms in play: calculate, explain, apply.
IGCSE (AO2)
"Calculate and comment" structure, worth 2–3 marks: show all working (method marks are awarded even if the final figure is wrong), then add one sentence of comment interpreting the value (e.g. "this means demand is elastic").
Practice move: give students a final PED answer with the working stripped out and have them reconstruct a valid 3-line "formula → substitution → answer + comment" solution that could have produced it.
IB (AO4)
Calculation technique extended into the value → implication chain: state the value, name the term (elastic/inelastic/unitary), then explicitly complete the sentence "Because PED = [value], a firm/government should therefore ___."
Practice move: take any IGCSE-style "calculate and comment" answer from this lesson and extend the final comment into a full firm-or-government policy recommendation sentence using the value → implication chain.
Extension Activities
  • Real-price hunt: find a real news story about a price change (fuel, transit fares, a subscription service) and estimate whether demand is likely elastic or inelastic using the determinants list, then predict the revenue effect.
  • Tax-incidence mini-case: [IB] research one real "sin tax" (tobacco, sugary drinks) and discuss, using PED reasoning, why governments often choose to tax goods with inelastic demand for revenue-raising purposes even though the stated goal is to reduce consumption.
  • PES bottleneck design: pick a real product and identify what would need to change (spare capacity, storage, factor mobility) to make its supply more elastic over time.

    Exam-Precise Glossary
    Price elasticity of demand (PED)%ΔQd ÷ %ΔP — measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in a good's own price.
    Price elasticity of supply (PES)%ΔQs ÷ %ΔP — measures how responsive quantity supplied is to a change in price.
    Perfectly inelastic / elastic|PED| = 0 (vertical curve, quantity never changes) / |PED| = ∞ (horizontal curve, any quantity sold only at one price).
    Unitary elasticity|PED| = 1 — % change in quantity exactly equals % change in price; total revenue unchanged by a small price change.
    Total revenuePrice × Quantity — visualised as the area of a rectangle under the demand curve at the chosen price.
    Determinants of PEDSubstitutes, necessity vs luxury, proportion of income, time period, habit/addiction, breadth of definition.
    Determinants of PESTime to produce, spare capacity, storability, factor mobility.
    Income elasticity of demand (YED)[IB enrichment] %ΔQd ÷ %ΔIncome — sign indicates normal (positive) vs inferior (negative) good.
    Tax incidence[IB HL enrichment] How the burden of a tax is split between buyers and sellers, determined by relative elasticity of demand and supply.
    Key Takeaways
    1. Elasticity calculation technique (show every step) is this lesson's core deliverable and reappears all course. 2. The revenue rule is conditional, not universal: it depends entirely on the PED value. 3. PES is PED's structural twin with different determinants — don't let students conflate the two determinant lists. 4. YED and tax incidence are genuine IB enrichment (YED sign; incidence-split calculation is HL only) — flag clearly as beyond IGCSE 0455. 5. The value → implication chain ("because PED = X, therefore...") is the AO4 skill examiners are actually testing, not the arithmetic alone.
