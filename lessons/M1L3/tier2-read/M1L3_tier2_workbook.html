5. "A firm should always raise its price if it wants to earn more revenue." Using the concept of price elasticity of demand, discuss whether this statement is true, referring to at least TWO different types of good in your answer. [8]
Answer Key & Mark Scheme Notes
1. %ΔQd = (220−300)/300 × 100 = −26.7%. %ΔP = (5−4)/4 × 100 = +25%. PED = −26.7 ÷ 25 = −1.07 (elastic, |PED| > 1). Comment: demand is relatively elastic — quantity demanded falls proportionally more than the price rise, so this price rise will reduce total revenue. [1 mark %ΔQd, 1 mark %ΔP, 1 mark correct ratio/value, 1 mark correct sign/rounding, 1 mark valid comment on elastic + revenue implication]
2. Two downward-sloping lines from a shared or nearby price axis: the ELASTIC curve should be drawn flatter/shallower (quantity changes a lot for a small price change); the INELASTIC curve should be drawn steeper (quantity barely changes even for a large price change). Both correctly labelled. [1 mark elastic flatter, 1 mark inelastic steeper, 1 mark both clearly labelled]
3. New revenue = $13 × 30 = $390 (down from $500 — a fall of $110). %ΔQd = (30−50)/50 × 100 = −40%. %ΔP = (13−10)/10 × 100 = +30%. PED = −40 ÷ 30 = −1.33 (elastic). Explanation: because |PED| > 1, quantity demanded fell proportionally MORE than price rose, so the loss of revenue from fewer scarves sold outweighed the gain from the higher price per scarf — this is exactly why raising price on an elastic good can reduce total revenue. [1 mark new revenue, 1 mark %ΔQd, 1 mark %ΔP, 1 mark PED value, 2 marks for explanation correctly linking the elastic value to the revenue fall]
4. %ΔQs = (480−400)/400 × 100 = +20%. %ΔP = (100−80)/80 × 100 = +25%. PES = 20 ÷ 25 = 0.8. Since 0 < PES < 1, supply is inelastic for this price change. [1 mark %ΔQs, 1 mark %ΔP, 1 mark correct PES value, 1 mark correct term "inelastic"]
5. Band 3 (6–8 marks): correctly identifies the statement as FALSE in general — whether raising price increases revenue depends entirely on PED. For a good with INELASTIC demand (e.g. petrol, insulin), raising price DOES increase total revenue because quantity demanded falls proportionally less than the price rise. For a good with ELASTIC demand (e.g. a branded snack with many close substitutes), raising price REDUCES total revenue because quantity demanded falls proportionally more than the price rise. A strong answer names two contrasting goods, links each to its likely elasticity value with a determinant-based reason (substitutes/necessity), and states the resulting revenue direction for each, ideally referencing the underlying %ΔQd vs %ΔP mechanism. Band 2 (3–5): correct general conclusion (depends on elasticity) with one worked example but the second good/case underdeveloped, or reasoning stated without linking clearly to the %-change mechanism. Band 1 (1–2): asserts the statement is simply true or false without using elasticity reasoning, or defines PED without applying it to the claim.
