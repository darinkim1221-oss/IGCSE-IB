Learning Objectives
By the end, students can…
explain, in plain language, why some prices swing wildly while others barely move
describe a good as "stretchy" or "stiff" based on how strongly buyers react to a price change
give a reason (substitutes, necessity, budget share) for why a good is stretchy or stiff
recognise that raising price does not always raise revenue — and explain why, using an example

Detailed Teaching Notes
Feel it before you name it
  • Hook: petrol-vs-haircut — let students guess before revealing anything.
  • Rubber band demo: the core metaphor. Candy (many competitors) "stretches" a lot; water in the desert (no substitute) stays "stiff." Keep returning to this metaphor — it's more durable for this age group than a verbal definition.
  • Lemonade revenue teaser + raise-price slider: revenue = price × quantity, and for a stretchy good, quantity can fall FASTER than price rises, so total revenue can fall. Use "total money made," not "revenue," if the class hasn't met the term.
  • Lemonade Elasticity Challenge (companion game file): a scored, replayable version of the same two-stall pattern — good in-class extension or homework kickoff.
Visuals & Diagrams
🎗️
The stretch meter: a simple horizontal bar that fills more when more buyers leave after a price rise. No axes or numbers — resist drawing a formal demand curve at this tier (that's the Tier 2 promotion).
Worked Example
Story: "A food-truck owner sells burritos on a street with five other food trucks. She raises her burrito price by $3 hoping to earn more. Instead, most of her lunch line walks two trucks down and her daily earnings actually fall." Walk through why: many nearby substitutes made her burritos "stretchy" — buyers left faster than the extra $3 could make up for.
Common Misconceptions
"Raising the price always means more money for the seller." Fix: use the Candy Corner numbers directly — quantity sold falls faster than price rises, so total money made goes down.
"Stretchy/stiff is just about how much you like something." Fix: it's really about whether you have anywhere ELSE to go — a good you love but that has many substitutes can still be "stretchy."
"Every good is either fully stretchy or fully stiff." Fix: it's a spectrum, not a switch — most goods sit somewhere in between.
Teaching Resources
Kid-Friendly Glossary
Stretchy (elastic)Buyers react a LOT to a price change — often because they have easy substitutes.
Stiff (inelastic)Buyers barely react to a price change — often because they have no substitute or truly need it.
SubstituteSomething else you could buy instead that does a similar job.
Total money made (revenue)Price × how many units are sold — can go up OR down when price changes, depending on how buyers react.
