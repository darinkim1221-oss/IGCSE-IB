Warm-up
Show what you discovered today
1. What does it mean for a good to be "stretchy" (elastic)? Circle the best answer.
  • It is very expensive.
  • Buyers react a lot to a price change — many switch away if it rises.
  • It never goes on sale.
2. Fill in the blank: A good is usually "stiff" (inelastic) when it has ______________ substitutes.
3. Why does petrol's price swing around a lot, but a haircut's price barely moves? Explain in 1–2 sentences.
4. Which stall could you raise the price on WITHOUT losing many buyers? Circle ONE, then give ONE reason.
  • Candy Corner
  • Desert Water
5. Match each product to STRETCHY or STIFF.
One brand of chips (many others nearby)
Insulin (life-saving medicine, no substitute)
Salt (tiny part of your budget, no real substitute)
A trendy toy with 5 similar copycats
6. For each item, write S for Stretchy or T for Stiff.
  • Coffee at your only campus café.
  • A specific candy bar surrounded by 20 others just like it.
  • Petrol for your family's only car.
  • One brand of notebook when the store sells ten similar ones.
7. Draw a bar graph. Draw one bar for how many candy buyers stayed after a price rise, and one bar for how many water buyers stayed after the same size price rise.

8. A shop owner raises the price of a stretchy good a lot and total revenue FALLS. Explain why this happens, using the word "buyers" in your answer.
🏠 Family Discussion Mission
Ask a family member: "Is there a product you'd keep buying almost no matter how much the price went up? What about one you'd drop the moment it got even a little more expensive?" Write down their two examples and why.
Answer Key
1. "Buyers react a lot to a price change — many switch away if it rises." — Elasticity is about buyer reaction, not price level.
2. Few / no. — With no other option, buyers keep buying even when price rises.
3. Sample: "Petrol has few substitutes in the short run (you still need to drive), but many barbers compete for haircuts, so that price stays steady." Accept reasoning built around substitutes/necessity.
4. Desert Water — because it has no substitute nearby, buyers keep paying even at a high price. Candy Corner's buyers flee to a competitor the moment price rises.
5. Chips: Stretchy. Insulin: Stiff. Salt: Stiff. Trendy toy with copycats: Stretchy.
6. T, S, T, S. — No nearby substitute or a true necessity makes something stiff; many similar options make it stretchy.
7. Accept any two bars showing candy buyers dropping much more sharply than water buyers — the key skill is drawing/reading a simple bar graph.
8. Accept: because so many buyers switch to a substitute or stop buying that the number of buyers falls by MORE than the price rose, so price × quantity (revenue) goes down overall.
